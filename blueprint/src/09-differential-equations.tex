\chapter{Linear Differential Equations}\label{ch:differential-equations}

Linear ODEs are built from finite computations, not from a completed space of
trajectories.  For
\[
  y'(t)=A(t)y(t)+b(t),
\]
the data are rational interval evaluators for \(A\) and \(b\).  At every
requested precision they produce finitely many ordered matrix products,
simplex enclosures, and a rational remainder interval.

\begin{verbatim}
coefficient boxes -> ordered words -> finite transition
                  -> Duhamel sum -> factorial remainder
\end{verbatim}

The scoped Lean entry point is
\texttt{ComputableAnalysis.DifferentialEquationsFoundation}.  Its algebra is
dimension-independent and uses only finite rational vectors and matrices.

\label{thm:finite-cramer-interface}

\[
  P_{m+n}=P_{m,n}P_m.

forcing gives the exact finite variation-of-constants formula.

Row and column absolute sums are finite rational bounds, sufficient to carry
state and forcing errors through these products.  No normed or completed
function space is needed.

For a constant coefficient, the ordered-simplex factor is generated by
\begin{verbatim}
V(0,T) := 1
V(r+1,T) := T/(r+1) * V(r,T)
\end{verbatim}
and is proved equal to \(T^r/r!\).  This finite identity explains the
factorial in the Peano--Baker expansion.

\section{Tail and uniqueness}

If \(M\) bounds the coefficient on an interval of length \(T\), terms after
degree \(r\) are enclosed by an explicit factorial majorant.  A finite search
chooses a shift making that bound smaller than any positive rational
tolerance.

The same estimate proves uniqueness.  If the pointwise error satisfies, for
every finite \(q\),
\[
  0\le e(x)\le B\frac{(MT)^{N+q}}{(N+q)!},
\]
then every requested positive tolerance eventually contains it, so its raw
interval computation is equivalent to zero.  This is the constructive
replacement for a supremum-norm argument.

Hence the intended solver is simply:

\begin{verbatim}
P_0(t)       := y_0
P_(r+1)(t)   := y_0 + integral_0^t (A(s) P_r(s) + b(s)) ds
answer       := P_r(t) +/- factorial_tail(M*T,r)
\end{verbatim}

Every displayed integral denotes a finite effective-FTC certificate over a
rectangle or ordered simplex.

\section{Remaining analytic provider}

The matrix algebra, factorial tail, and error-closing argument are formalized.
The missing general theorem is the function-specific provider that converts
coefficient evaluators into compatible interval-valued simplex computations.
It must supply:

\begin{verbatim}
componentwise coefficient/forcing regularity
    -> finite simplex enclosures
    -> uniform factorial-tail certificate
    -> initial-value transport and raw overlap
\end{verbatim}

its evaluators, simplex bounds, and tail schedule; it does not introduce a
second theory of integration or appeal to completeness of standard real
