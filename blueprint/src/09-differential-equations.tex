\chapter{Linear Differential Equations}\label{ch:differential-equations}

Linear ODEs are the first large consumer of the preceding machinery. We do
not postulate a completed space of trajectories: a requested precision is met
by a finite matrix computation together with a rational error certificate.

For $y'(t)=A(t)y(t)+b(t)$, the coefficient and forcing entries are
interval-certified computations on a common rational domain. The effective FTC
supplies the simplex integrals; this chapter supplies their finite matrix
assembly and uniqueness principle.

The analytic hand-off is the same one used by every earlier chapter:

\begin{verbatim}
coefficient/forcing evaluator -> cell or simplex bounds
                              -> effective FTC endpoint certificate
                              -> finite transition and Duhamel boxes
\end{verbatim}

The finite box part is now dimension-independent.  A separable coefficient
product can be assembled as a weighted nested rational sum and factored into
one finite sum per coordinate.  This supplies the rectangular component of a
multiple integral; chronological ordering and simplex restrictions remain
explicit additional data, rather than being hidden inside a Fubini or measure
theorem.

\section{Construction}
\label{thm:finite-cramer-interface}
\label{thm:finite-cayley-hamilton-boundary}

\begin{verbatim}
initial state -> sampled matrices -> ordered words -> tail bound -> enclosure
\end{verbatim}
The Lean layer defines rational vectors and matrices, executable finite sums,
chronological products, and the noncommutative Peano--Baker word expansion.
The order is part of the theorem: later transitions multiply on the left.
Cramer's rule handles finite solves; Cayley--Hamilton supplies recurrences for
small fixed dimensions.

The tail is not an informal limit. If the coefficient bound is $M$ on a
time interval of length $T$, the omitted degree-$r$ words are charged to
the finite factorial majorant $(MT)^r/r!$. An executable shift chooses the
first stage below any requested positive rational tolerance.

The reusable uniqueness certificate is equally finite.  A candidate error is
required to satisfy, for every iteration count (q),

\[
  0\le e(x)\le B\,\frac{(MT)^{N+q}}{(N+q)!}.
\]

The computable factorial-tail shift makes the right side smaller than any
positive rational tolerance; therefore the error is exactly zero.  This is
the pointwise replacement for taking a supremum norm in a completed function
space.

The construction is therefore a family of finite approximants:

\begin{verbatim}
P_0(t) = y_0
P_(r+1)(t) = y_0 + integral_0^t (A(s) P_r(s) + b(s)) ds
return P_r(t) +/- factorial_tail(M*T,r)
\end{verbatim}

Every integral in this pseudocode is an effective-FTC certificate on a finite
simplex or rectangle. The matrix products are finite ordered words; no
infinite matrix, function space, or completed trajectory is introduced.

\section{Evolution and uniqueness}

Mesh transitions give a finite variation-of-constants construction:
\begin{verbatim}
mesh -> local transitions -> compatible prefixes -> overlap -> trajectory box
\end{verbatim}

The equality principle is pointwise overlap of raw intervals.  The scalar
self-derivative case is already closed by a finite mesh-halving certificate:
subtract two candidates, repeatedly halve a rational error envelope, and use
the explicit tolerance schedule to force the error to zero.  For vector
systems, the Lean certificate is modular: the finite Peano--Baker layer closes
the argument once the analytic provider has supplied the coefficient bound and
the iterated error estimate.

For $y'=y$, the error envelope is scalar and the factorial estimate is
explicit. This gives uniqueness from the same finite data used to construct
the solution. The vector theorem has the identical shape; its remaining
provider fields are only matrix-norm bounds, coefficient regularity, and the
finite simplex tail estimate.

\section{Special cases}

For constant generators, matrix words reduce to powers. For rotations, even
and odd words split into cosine and sine prefixes, connecting the ODE chapter
back to the circle construction. Scalar $y'=y$ is the first uniqueness
case; affine forcing then gives variation of constants.
\label{thm:linear-ode-elementary-function-agreement}

The constant-coefficient and rotation algebra is checked before any analytic
series theorem:

For a constant generator, the ordered-simplex volume is built recursively:

\begin{verbatim}
V(0,T) := 1
V(r+1,T) := T/(r+1) * V(r,T)
V(r,T)  = T^r/r!
\end{verbatim}

This is a finite rational identity.  It explains the factorial coefficient in
the Peano--Baker terms before any infinite series or completed function space

\section{Finite complex paths}

\begin{verbatim}
path -> segment increments -> finite sum -> endpoint certificate
\end{verbatim}
Complex-valued functions are supported from the start by pairing rational real
and imaginary interval computations. Polygonal paths give finite
zero-displacement identities for the constant and polynomial differentials
covered here; analytic path-independence enters only after the corresponding
finite subdivision certificate is supplied.

The public complex layer is therefore small but complete at its finite core:
boxes enclose rational-complex values, polygonal left sums are assembled at a
requested evaluator stage, and closed-path exactness is proved for constant
and polynomial differentials. This is the complex counterpart of the real
FTC contract, with path subdivision replacing interval subdivision.
