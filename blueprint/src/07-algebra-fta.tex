\section{The finite meaning of ``find a root''}

The project does not begin with an abstract complex plane and then invoke the
fundamental theorem of algebra.  It begins with a finite search contract:

\begin{verbatim}
  candidate box -> polynomial interval image
                  -> reject / subdivide / retain
                  -> residual certificate
\end{verbatim}

For a real interval, sign information and Descartes counts are finite
exclusion data.  For a complex box, the same role is played by a rational
complex residual bound.  A root is therefore a retained box plus a proof that
every requested stage remains compatible with the polynomial evaluation; the
box need not contain an attained irrational point.

quadratic certificate identifies the two supplied complex roots and excludes

\section{Complex boxes as a finite search}

The complex version makes the stage parameter visible. Start with a rational
rectangle, subdivide it into finitely many children, and retain only children
whose polynomial image is still compatible with zero:

\begin{verbatim}
initial QBox -> finite subdivision at depth d
             -> reject root-free boxes
             -> retain boxes containing supplied roots
             -> width = initial width / 2^d
\end{verbatim}

For the quadratic model, the upper and lower roots survive every displayed
depth while a neighborhood of zero is excluded. This is a genuine shrinking
complex `realRaw`-style computation: it records boxes and residual tests, not
an assertion that an irrational complex coordinate has been attained.

\section{Boundary}

The supplied-root quintic certificate is a boundary result: it peels known
factors, but is not a general quintic solver.

For the worked polynomial with supplied roots (-2,-1,0,1,2), the chain
records the coefficients, transports each remaining root through the next
synthetic quotient, and ends with a constant deflated polynomial:

\begin{verbatim}
five supplied roots -> five finite quotient steps
                   -> Horner factorization
                   -> exact product identity
\end{verbatim}

This is the algebraic analogue of choosing a known computational branch. It
is useful downstream when a factor is already certified by geometry or an
earlier inverse search; it does not manufacture the roots. The general FTA
and general quintic solver remain outside the current constructive boundary.
