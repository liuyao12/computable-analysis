\section{Scope and foundation}

This subproject uses the project's rational interval algorithms, computable
complex values, and explicit finite-difference derivative certificates.
There is no Mathlib real-number dependency. The first checked milestone
concerns the Euler--Fuchs equation and Painlev\'e I and II; the general
Fuchs--Painlev\'e classification remains a research goal.

\begin{definition}[Polynomial differential residuals]
\label{def:algebraic-ode-residual}
A finite expression in $x,y,y',y''$, with rational constants, addition,
negation and multiplication, can be evaluated over rational numbers,
rational complex numbers, or raw computable complex algorithms.
An algebraic relation in $x,y$ includes a rational evaluation witness proving
that its polynomial is not identically zero.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Expr}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.AlgebraicRelation}
\leanok
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Represented residual semantics]
\label{thm:algebraic-ode-runtime}
Evaluating a polynomial expression at valid raw inputs yields a valid raw
output and respects overlap equivalence. At exact rational inputs its
runtime is the exact rational evaluation at every stage.
This is a residual theorem: derivative certificates remain separate.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Expr.evalRaw_valid}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Expr.evalRaw_equiv}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Expr.evalRaw_ofRat}
\leanok
\end{theorem}

\section{Forward Fuchs growth without completed reals}

\begin{definition}[Local differential solution on rational boxes]
\label{def:fuchs-local-solution}
For a rational matrix function $A(t)$ on $0<t\le R$, a solution gives valid
component interval algorithms $Y(t)$. For every closed rational interval
$[a,b]\subset(0,R]$ and positive rational $\varepsilon$, it gives a strictly
positive rational step radius and a step-dependent precision stage. For
all sufficiently refined box samples $v$ at $x$ and $w$ at $x+h$,
\[
 \|w-v-hA(x)v\|_1\le\varepsilon|h|.
\]
No growth bound is part of this definition. A represented norm bound $M$
means that each stage box meets the rational norm ball of radius $M$;
the nearest-to-zero vector tests this using finite rational arithmetic.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.LinearODE.LinearSolution}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.LinearODE.NormBound}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.LinearODE.normBound_exact}
\leanok
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial growth from a simple matrix pole]
\label{thm:fuchs-matrix-growth}
Suppose $t\sum_i|A_{ij}(t)|\le N$ for every column and $0<t\le R$, with
$N$ a natural number. Every local differential solution satisfies
\[
 \|Y(a)\|_1\le\frac{b^N M}{a^N},\qquad 0<a\le b\le R,
\]
where $M$ is computed as the sum of absolute endpoints of the stage-zero
component boxes at $b$. Thus the outer constant requires no supremum.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.LinearODE.normBound_from_initial_boxes}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.LinearODE.LinearSolution.moderate_growth}
\leanok
\end{theorem}

The proof uses the finite polynomial inequality
$s^N(t+N(t-s))\le t^{N+1}$ for $0\le s\le t$, an explicit finite rational
mesh, and elimination of an arbitrary rational error budget. No completed
real line, integral, logarithm or exponential is introduced.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.LinearODE.weighted_step_factor}
\leanok

\begin{theorem}[Forward Fuchs theorem along a rational ray]
\label{thm:fuchs-ray-growth}
Let $p,q$ be complex rational polynomials, and let $z=td$ for a fixed
nonzero rational complex direction. The scaled jet $Y=(y,zy')$ of
$z^2y''+zp(z)y'+q(z)y=0$ has matrix
\[
 A(t)=\frac1t\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-q(td)&1-p(td)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A Horner absolute-coefficient estimate supplies an integer $N$ satisfying
the preceding column bound. Every solution of this certified differential
system therefore has polynomial growth along the ray.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Growth.companion_apply}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Growth.rayMatrix_pole_bound}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Growth.fuchs_ray_moderate}
\leanok
\end{theorem}

The reciprocal example $y=1/t$ for $p=2,q=0$ is constructed from the
existing finite-difference derivative certificate; its bound is $4/a^4$
for $0<a\le1$. A non-singleton Bessel-factor evaluator also gives a
constant solution of $Y'=0$. These exercise the solution definition
independently of the growth conclusion.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Growth.reciprocal_moderate}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Tests.besselConstant_growth}
\leanok

This is the forward implication for polynomial regular coefficients on
rational rays. A general holomorphic-solution adapter, uniform sector bounds,
arbitrary holomorphic coefficient germs, and the converse criterion remain
open. It is independent of the formal Frobenius construction below.

\section{Euler--Fuchs polynomial solutions}

\begin{theorem}[Coefficient classification]
\label{thm:fuchs-polynomial-classification}
For $xy'=r y$ and a finite rational polynomial $p=\sum_k c_kx^k$,
the coefficient equations are $(k-r)c_k=0$ for every $k$.
They hold exactly when each nonzero coefficient has $k=r$, and imply
$x p'(x)=r p(x)$ under the existing executable polynomial derivative.
In particular, a nonzero polynomial solution forces $r$ to be a
nonnegative integer.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.polynomial_classification}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.polynomial_equation}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.exponent_of_nonzero_polynomial}
\leanok
\end{theorem}

For a radical relation $y^n=x^m$, the checked algebraic elimination derives
$nxy'=my$ from the differentiated relation, provided $y\ne0$ and $n\ne0$.
It does not construct a branch or prove implicit differentiation.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.radical_euler}
\leanok

\section{Painlev\'e equations and algebraic seeds}

We use the DLMF convention
\[
  \mathrm{P_I}: y''=6y^2+x,\qquad
  \mathrm{P_{II}}(\alpha): y''=2y^3+xy+\alpha.
\]
PI has no rational constant solution. For PII, the only rational affine
solution is $y=0$, at $\alpha=0$.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Painleve.first_no_constant}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Painleve.affine_classification}
\leanok

\begin{theorem}[Simple-pole classification and analytic certificates]
\label{thm:painleve-simple-pole}
On the punctured rational line, $y=c/x$, with
$y'=-c/x^2$ and $y''=2c/x^3$, satisfies PII exactly when
\[
  \alpha=-c,\qquad c\in\{0,1,-1\}.
\]
On every positive rational interval the constructed solution package
contains both two-sided finite-difference derivative certificates, the ODE
identity, and the nonzero algebraic relation $xy-c=0$ for the same evaluator.
The represented-complex residual also vanishes at every nonzero rational
input. Thus these are checked algebraic seeds, not merely assigned jets.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Painleve.simple_pole_classification}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Painleve.poleSolution}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Tests.negativePole_represented}
\leanok
\end{theorem}

The sign symmetry and the Riccati reduction at $\alpha=1/2$ are checked
algebraic identities. The latter explicitly assumes differentiated Riccati
data. Arbitrary-degree polynomial, rational, and algebraic solution
classifications have not yet been proved.

\section{Formal Frobenius construction}

For rational polynomial coefficients $p,q$, consider the local form
\[
 x^2y''+xp(x)y'+q(x)y=0,\qquad I(s)=s(s-1)+p_0s+q_0.
\]
The rational exponent $r$ is a formal shift. Finite Cauchy products and
shifted Euler operators define the residual independently of its solver.

\begin{theorem}[Indicial obstruction and nonresonant construction]
\label{thm:frobenius-formal}
Coefficient extraction gives
\[
 I(r+n)c_n+\sum_{k<n}\bigl((r+k)p_{n-k}+q_{n-k}\bigr)c_k=0.
\]
A nonzero $c_0$ forces $I(r)=0$. If $I(r)=0$ and $I(r+n)\ne0$ for every
positive integer $n$, a terminating rational coefficient algorithm constructs
the unique formal solution with prescribed $c_0$. Every finite prefix has
zero residual coefficients below its cutoff. No analytic convergence or
branch of $x^r$ is asserted by this formal theorem.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.residual_split}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.indicial_obstruction}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.coeff_isSolution}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.solution_unique}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.truncation_residual}
\leanok
\end{theorem}

At a resonant index, the lower contribution must vanish. For example,
$x^2y''+xy=0$ at exponent zero forces $c_0=0$ at degree one; division by a
zero indicial coefficient cannot silently construct the desired solution.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.resonance_compatibility}
\leanok

\begin{theorem}[Effective convergence for nonnegative root gap]
\label{thm:frobenius-convergence}
Suppose $I(r)=0$ and $A=2r-1+p_0\ge0$. Put
\[
 R=1+(|r|+1)\sum_j|p_j|+\sum_j|q_j|.
\]
Then $I(r+n)=n(n+A)\ge n^2$ for positive $n$, and the constructed
coefficients satisfy $|c_n|\le |c_0|R^n$. For rational $x$ with
$R|x|\le 1/2$, let $S_n(x)=\sum_{k<n}c_kx^k$. The literal boxes
\[
 [S_n(x)-2|c_0|2^{-n},\ S_n(x)+2|c_0|2^{-n}]
\]
are ordered and nested, contain all later finite partial sums, and have
width $4|c_0|2^{-n}$. A requested positive rational width has an explicit
computation stage. Thus the Frobenius factor is a valid represented value.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.indicial_lower_bound}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.coeff_growth}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.factorRaw_valid}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.factorRaw_contains_prefix}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Frobenius.Equation.factorRaw_precision}
\leanok
\end{theorem}

The reusable geometric-tail evaluator works for signed series. This theorem
includes larger rational roots and repeated roots; it does not construct
$x^r$, certify analytic differentiation, or cover smaller roots with negative
gap. For the modified Bessel equation $x^2y''+xy'-x^2y=0$, it certifies
$|x|\le1/6$; twelve terms at $x=1/8$ give width $1/1024$.

\begin{theorem}[Terminating Laguerre algebraic solutions]
\label{thm:laguerre-polynomial}
For every natural $m$, rational $b>0$, and initial value $c_0$, the recurrence
\[
 c_{n+1}=\frac{(n-m)c_n}{(n+1)(n+b)}
\]
terminates above degree $m$ and gives a polynomial solving
$xy''+(b-x)y'+my=0$ at every rational input, including zero.
If $c_0\ne0$, its degree is exactly $m$. Both two-sided finite-difference
derivative certificates are constructed on every supplied bounded rational
interval, and the same evaluator satisfies the nonzero graph relation
$y-P(x)=0$. This is local regular-singular theory at zero; infinity is
irregular for the Laguerre equation.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Laguerre.polynomial_exact_degree}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Laguerre.differential_equation}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Laguerre.firstDerivativeCertificate}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Laguerre.secondDerivativeCertificate}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.AlgebraicODE.Fuchs.Laguerre.algebraic_relation}
\leanok
\end{theorem}

\section{Lean 4 comparison and next milestones}

\href{https://github.com/zinan-huang/Ripple/tree/e9ce148d3975f9e75bb9724e01ec763ad9b368a9/Ripple/Number/Frobenius}
{Ripple's Lean 4 Frobenius development} is the closest linear comparison.
Its indicial obstruction, formal nonresonant solution, and uniqueness can be
compared directly to our statements. Ripple also supplies higher-order and
analytic-convergence results beyond the current general construction here.
It uses Mathlib's real numbers; our construction uses rational coefficients
and finite interval certificates. The pinned source was inspected, not
independently rebuilt.

\href{https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/ODE/ExistUnique.html}
{Mathlib's ODE existence and uniqueness} provides interface comparisons;
\href{https://github.com/seewoo5/lean-poly-abc}{Mason--Stothers in Lean 4}
provides adjacent polynomial derivative and degree arguments. No public Lean 4
Painlev\'e algebraic-solution classification was located in the bounded search.
Coq/Rocq and Isabelle work is background, not the direct comparison benchmark.

Next are analytic differentiation, convergence for the remaining nonresonant
roots, certified algebraic branches and
Puiseux charts, and resonant logarithmic solutions. Painlev\'e work remains
separate: pole/degree obstructions, B\"acklund transformations and rational
solution classification for PII, followed by III--VI. Global monodromy and
the general algebraic classification remain open.

The new modules add no custom axioms or proof placeholders. Exact
classification proofs use standard Lean logical axioms; raw validity and
derivative packages inherit the foundation's existing native-decision
dependencies, exposed by the subproject's axiom audit.
