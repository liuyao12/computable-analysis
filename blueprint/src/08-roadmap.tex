\chapter{Formalization Roadmap}

\section{What counts as progress}

The project should prefer theorem statements that end in one of the following
finite conclusions:
\[
  \RealRaw.\Valid,\qquad
  \ComplexRaw.\Valid,\qquad
  x\Equiv y,\qquad
  \close_\eps(I,J).
\]
These are the computational substitutes for classical existence and equality.
Whenever a classical proof says "choose a sufficiently fine partition" or
"take a small enough neighborhood", the blueprint should say whether this is
an exact existence step using raw-real completeness, or a later executable
algorithm requiring an explicit modulus.

\section{Near-term milestones}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Finish the exact order layer:
  prove the remaining order laws for \cref{def:raw-real-order}, especially
  transitivity and compatibility with raw-real arithmetic under valid
  representatives.

  \item Formalize raw-real completeness:
  construct exact raw reals from nested rational intervals, then use this to
  define infima and suprema of bounded monotone rational families.

  \item Define one-sided convex derivatives:
  formalize \cref{def:convex-right-derivative} and
  \cref{def:convex-left-derivative} as infimum and supremum constructions for
  rational secants of a \cref{def:exact-convexity} function.

  \item Prove monotone integrability:
  use the definite-integral construction from Chapter~\ref{ch:integrals} to
  prove \cref{thm:monotone-integrable}.

  \item Close the convex FTC:
  prove \cref{thm:convex-ftc} from convexity, one-sided derivatives,
  monotone integrability, and the telescoping secant inequalities.

  \item Prove the interval arithmetic formula-identification rules:
  sums, scalar multiples, products, and reciprocals should transform
  formula-identification cell controls into new cell controls.

  \item Finish geometric arctangent cell control:
  prove the finite sector-area inequalities in \cref{ex:arctan-ftc} and use
  them to construct the kernel comparison route for \(1/(1+x^2)\).

  \item Derive arctangent Taylor soundness from FTC:
  combine geometric arctangent cell control with the finite kernel remainder
  identity to prove that the geometric and power-series arctangents agree on
  \([0,1]\), giving the analytic bridge needed for pi.

  \item Finish geometric pi validity:
  the area loop now has a direct validity proof; finish circumference validity
  and the polygon bridge needed for \cref{thm:geometric-pi-validity}.

  \item Choose an FTA route:
  either develop algebraic complex numbers with isolation boxes, or develop
  the argument-principle box-search route.
\end{enumerate}

as definition-complete only after its interval sequence has been verified as a
valid raw real: each interval is ordered, the sequence is nested, and the
widths shrink to zero.  Equivalence counts when there is a formalized chain of
\code{RealRaw.Equiv} theorems connecting the computation to
\code{piCircleArea}.  For formulas that compute a related quantity first, such
as \(\zeta(2)\) or \(\sqrt{2\pi}\), the definition column records whether the
displayed interval algorithm is verified, while the equivalence column records
whether the corresponding \(\pi\)-formula has been connected back to
\code{piCircleArea}.

\section{A useful style rule}

Every major theorem should have three layers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a readable mathematical statement in the blueprint;
  \item a Lean proposition or structure with the same quantifier shape;
  \item small finite lemmas that discharge the numerical or algebraic
  estimates.
\end{enumerate}
This keeps the project faithful to the historical mathematics while making
the formal work genuinely computable.
