\chapter{Formalization Roadmap}

The blueprint is a map, not a second proof. Figures show the computation;
pseudocode names the invariant; Lean carries the details.

\section{Admission rule}

Include a result when it yields a rational certificate, a shrinking `realRaw`,
a valid `real`, or an overlap between independent computations. Reuse routine
finite combinatorics from the library.

\section{Order}

\begin{verbatim}
foundations -> geometry -> rectangles -> series and pi
            -> MVT/FTC -> algebraic isolation -> linear ODEs
\end{verbatim}

\section{List of 100 Theorem Formalization Challenge}
\label{sec:100-theorem-alignment}

The preface indexes the scoped entries and links each to its blueprint
statement. The list is a progress scoreboard, not a promise to reproduce the
unrestricted classical theorem.

The authoritative computable proxy currently contains 52 scoped entries. Its
Lean registry, including machine-checked coverage of the benchmark numbers,
is 	exttt{ComputableAnalysis/WiedijkScoreboard.lean}. Each entry records the
project-native replacement: a finite certificate, a raw-real equivalence, or
an explicitly named constructive substitute when the unrestricted theorem
requires classical completeness or another out-of-scope object.

\section{Style}

Use only `realRaw` and `real` when the distinction from standard real numbers
matters; leave implementation names and proof details to Lean.
