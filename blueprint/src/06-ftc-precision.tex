\section{The FTC stated only with rational endpoint pairs}
\label{sec:ftc-finite-output}

The literal objects are algorithms with two rational outputs:
\[
 f_n(x)=[f_n^-(x),f_n^+(x)],\qquad
 g_n(x)=[g_n^-(x),g_n^+(x)].
\]
Here $x$ is a permitted rational input and $n$ a natural-number stage.
There is no scalar evaluation $f(x)$ or $g(x)$ in the definitions below.
Fixing $x$ leaves a program in $n$, not an additional scalar output.
The unindexed formulas earlier in the chapter describe the intended
relations; this section gives their endpoint-only statements and proofs.

\subsection{Validity, compatibility, and removing rational slack}
Validity of an evaluator means that, for each permitted rational $x$,
\[
 f_n^-(x)\le f_m^-(x)\le f_m^+(x)\le f_n^+(x)\quad(n\le m),
 \qquad
 \forall\varepsilon\in\Q_{>0}\ \exists n,\quad
 f_n^+(x)-f_n^-(x)<\varepsilon.
\]
The same requirements apply to $g$.  Two evaluators agree at a rational
input precisely when
\[
 f_n^-(x)\le g_m^+(x),\qquad
 g_m^-(x)\le f_n^+(x)\qquad\text{for every }n,m.
\]
These are comparisons between four rational numbers.  No common point
of the infinitely many intervals is introduced.

We repeatedly use the following rational fact.  If fixed rationals
$r,s$ satisfy $r\le s+e_k$ for every $k$, where nonnegative rational
errors $e_k$ become arbitrarily small, then $r\le s$.  Otherwise the
positive rational gap $r-s$ determines a stage giving a contradiction.
For finitely many valid evaluations, the sum of their widths can likewise
be made smaller than any given rational tolerance by taking a common
sufficiently large stage.  We call this \emph{removing rational slack}.
It is not a completeness theorem.

For example, suppose a fixed rational pair $[L,U]$ overlaps every
$g_n(x)$.  This means
\[
 L\le g_n^+(x),\qquad g_n^-(x)\le U\quad\text{for all }n.
\]
It does not mean that every $g_n(x)$ is contained in $[L,U]$.
If a second pair $[L',U']$ has the same property, then
$L\le U'+(g_n^+(x)-g_n^-(x))$ for all $n$.  Removing slack proves
$L\le U'$, and symmetrically $L'\le U$.  Thus the two pairs overlap.

\subsection{A derivative relation with no scalar evaluations}
All pair arithmetic in this subsection is finite rational interval
arithmetic.  Define the residual pair $R_n(x,h)$ by its endpoints:
\begin{align*}
 R_n^-(x,h)&=f_n^-(x+h)-f_n^+(x)
                   -\max(hg_n^-(x),hg_n^+(x)),\\
 R_n^+(x,h)&=f_n^+(x+h)-f_n^-(x)
                   -\min(hg_n^-(x),hg_n^+(x)).
\end{align*}
We say that the evaluator $g$ differentiates the evaluator $f$ at $x$ if
\[
 \forall\varepsilon\in\Q_{>0}\ \exists\delta\in\Q_{>0}\ 
 \forall h\in\Q,\quad
 0<|h|<\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ 
 \exists n:\quad
 -\varepsilon|h|\le R_n^-(x,h)\le R_n^+(x,h)\le\varepsilon|h|,
\]
with $x+h$ restricted to the domain.  Once the endpoint test holds, it
holds at all later stages by nesting.  The stage is allowed to depend
on $h$: precision must increase when division by a smaller increment
amplifies the output widths.  This condition is neither a uniform
stage requirement nor the assertion that a finite difference pair is
identical to a derivative pair.

Derivative uniqueness is also an endpoint theorem.  For two derivative
evaluators choose an increment small enough for both residual estimates,
and refine to a common stage.  The nonempty rational difference-quotient
pair then lies within the prescribed rational tolerance of both derivative
pairs.  Two fixed disjoint derivative pairs would have a positive rational
gap; choosing the tolerance smaller than one quarter of that gap contradicts
the estimates after refinement.  Consequently the derivative evaluators'
pairs overlap at every pair of stages.  This argument uses only rational
midpoints of finite intervals, not a derivative scalar.

\subsection{Convexity and the derivative constructor}
Fix rational $A<a<b<B$.  The evaluator $f$ is convex on $[A,B]$ when
\[
 f_n^-((1-t)x+ty)
       \le (1-t)f_m^+(x)+t f_k^+(y)
\]
for every rational $x,y\in[A,B]$, rational $0\le t\le1$, and
all stages $n,m,k$.  Convexity is a property proved about the evaluator;
it need not hold separately for its two endpoint functions.

At a rational $x\in[a,b]$, set
\[
 h_0(x)=\tfrac12\min(x-A,B-x),\qquad h_j(x)=2^{-j}h_0(x).
\]
Search for a common stage $\nu_j$ at which the three output widths at
$x-h_j,x,x+h_j$ are at most $h_j2^{-j-3}$.  Write those outputs as
$[a_-,b_-],[a_0,b_0],[a_+,b_+]$, respectively, and set
\[
 B_j(x)=\left[\frac{a_0-b_-}{h_j},\frac{b_+-a_0}{h_j}\right],
 \qquad
 d_j(x)=\left[\max_{k\le j}B_k^-(x),\min_{k\le j}B_k^+(x)\right].
\]
The three value-width searches terminate by validity of $f$.
Convexity, with rational weights chosen at the three secant endpoints,
proves that every lower endpoint of a $B_j$ is at most every upper
endpoint of a $B_k$.  Thus $d_j$ is a nonempty rational pair and the
$d_j$ are nested.  No scalar derivative was used as a witness.

The constructor has the following explicit termination hypothesis:
\[
 \forall x\in[a,b]\cap\Q\ \forall\varepsilon\in\Q_{>0}\ 
 \exists j,\qquad B_j^+(x)-B_j^-(x)<\varepsilon.
\]
Under it, $d$ is valid and differentiates $f$ in the residual-pair sense.
Indeed convexity bounds every sufficiently short rational secant between
the chosen outer secants, up to the finitely many evaluation widths.
Choose $B_j$ narrower than a fraction of the requested tolerance, then
refine the outputs at $x,x+h$ until the extra residual widths are smaller
than that tolerance times $|h|$.  This proves the residual endpoint test.

Conversely, if a valid supplied $g$ differentiates a convex $f$, apply its
residual bounds to $h_j$ and $-h_j$ at a common refined stage.  They bound
the refined secant gap by twice the slope tolerance plus the width of
$g$ at $x$.  Returning to the stage $\nu_j$ adds at most $2^{-j-1}$:
this is the sum of the four endpoint errors divided by $h_j$.
First choose the slope tolerance, then $j$ sufficiently large and a
sufficiently narrow $g$ output.  Removing the remaining refinement slack
proves the displayed termination condition.  Derivative uniqueness
identifies $d$ and $g$ by cross-stage overlap.

\subsection{The finite secant inequalities}
Under convexity and the derivative relation, for rational $u<v$,
all derivative stages $k$, and all function stages $m$, one has
\begin{align*}
 (v-u)g_k^-(u)&\le f_m^+(v)-f_m^-(u),\\
 f_m^-(v)-f_m^+(u)&\le(v-u)g_k^+(v).
\end{align*}
To prove these, compare a small secant at the appropriate endpoint with
the secant from $u$ to $v$ using the endpoint convexity inequality.
The residual-pair relation supplies the derivative comparison at refined
stages.  Coarse endpoints can only weaken these bounds.  The residual
tolerance and finitely many refined output widths can be made arbitrarily
small, so removing rational slack gives exactly the two inequalities above.
Applying both at arbitrarily fine function stages also gives
\[
 g_k^-(u)\le g_l^+(v)\qquad\text{for all }k,l.
\]
This is the endpoint-only meaning of a nondecreasing derivative evaluator.

\subsection{An independent integral algorithm}
The construction in this subsection needs only a valid $g$ satisfying the
last monotonicity inequalities.  It does not read $f$.
Put $L=b-a$ and compute the nonnegative rational
\[
 V=g_0^+(b)-g_0^-(a).
\]
For a positive rational $\varepsilon$, choose an integer $N\ge1$ with
$LV/N\le\varepsilon/2$, and choose $0<\theta\le\varepsilon/(2L)$.
At the equal partition $x_i=a+iL/N$, search for stages $k_i$ with
\[
 g_{k_i}(x_i)=[\alpha_i,\beta_i],\qquad \beta_i-\alpha_i\le\theta.
\]
Each cell range $[\alpha_i,\beta_{i+1}]$ overlaps every $g_m(t)$ for
rational $x_i\le t\le x_{i+1}$, by monotonicity.  Output the rational pair
\[
 C(\varepsilon)=\left[
    \frac LN\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\alpha_i,
    \frac LN\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\beta_{i+1}\right].
\]
Its width has the \emph{exact finite identity}
\[
 C^+-C^-=\frac LN\left(\beta_N-\alpha_0
                   +\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(\beta_i-\alpha_i)\right)
        \le\frac{LV}{N}+L\theta\le\varepsilon.
\]
Endpoint nesting gives $\beta_N-\alpha_0\le V$.  No differences
of uncomputed scalars occur in this identity.

Two different partitions' cell ranges overlap on every common refined
cell: choose a rational sample there and apply the rational-slack lemma
to its shrinking $g$ outputs.  Weighted addition proves that the two
integral pairs overlap.  For $C_j=C(2^{-j})$, define
\[
 J_n=\left[\max_{j\le n}C_j^-,\min_{j\le n}C_j^+\right].
\]
Finite pairwise overlap proves $J_n^-\le J_n^+$, nesting is explicit,
and $J_n^+-J_n^-\le2^{-n}$.  This is integral validity independently
of a primitive.  The same common-refinement proof identifies this
algorithm with any other sound shrinking cell-range integral algorithm.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint-only fundamental theorem]
\label{thm:ftc-finite-output}
Let $f,g$ be valid rational-pair evaluators on the domains above.  Suppose
$f$ satisfies the endpoint convexity inequalities and $g$ differentiates
$f$ by the residual-pair relation.  The derivative constructor from $f$
is valid and agrees with $g$.  The independent integral constructor from
$g$ is valid.  Its outputs satisfy, for every $n,m\in\N$,
\[
 \boxed{J_n^-\le f_m^+(b)-f_m^-(a),\qquad
        f_m^-(b)-f_m^+(a)\le J_n^+.}
\]
Thus every integral-output pair overlaps every endpoint-difference pair
\[
 E_m=[f_m^-(b)-f_m^+(a),\ f_m^+(b)-f_m^-(a)].
\]
These two rational inequalities are the FTC conclusion.  An equality
between unspecified scalar values is not part of the statement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a finite partition used in an output $C_j$ and a function stage $s$.
The finite secant inequalities give
\[
 C_j^-\le f_s^+(b)-f_s^-(a)
          +\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(f_s^+(x_i)-f_s^-(x_i)),
\]
and
\[
 f_s^-(b)-f_s^+(a)
          -\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(f_s^+(x_i)-f_s^-(x_i))\le C_j^+.
\]
\emph{Finite interval differences do not telescope exactly.}  The
interior widths displayed here are the terms left after cancellation
of equal rational endpoints.  For any fixed target stage $m$, take
$s\ge m$.  The endpoint terms are then bounded by $E_m^+$ and $E_m^-$,
respectively, and the finite sum of interior widths can be made smaller
than every positive rational tolerance.  Removing slack gives
$C_j^-\le E_m^+$ and $E_m^-\le C_j^+$.
Taking the finite maximum and minimum over $j\le n$ proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

Both $E$ and $J$ have shrinking widths.  If their chosen widths add to
at most $\eta$, their overlap shows that the rational pair
\[
 [\min(J_n^-,E_m^-),\max(J_n^+,E_m^+)]
\]
has width at most $\eta$.  This is a further finite accuracy comparison
between the two algorithms, not an extraction of a common limiting value.

\subsection{Differences of convex evaluators}
Let $p,q$ be convex pair evaluators with terminating derivative constructors
$d^p,d^q$.  Supply $f$ whose outputs agree, by cross-stage overlap, with
\[
 [p_n^-(x)-q_n^+(x),\ p_n^+(x)-q_n^-(x)].
\]
The pairs
$[d_n^{p,-}(x)-d_n^{q,+}(x),d_n^{p,+}(x)-d_n^{q,-}(x)]$
define a derivative evaluator for $f$.  Any independently supplied
derivative evaluator $g$ agrees with it by residual-pair uniqueness.

At partition endpoints compute derivative pairs
$[\alpha_i^p,\beta_i^p]$ and $[\alpha_i^q,\beta_i^q]$, each of width
at most $\theta$.  The cell ranges for $g$ are
\[
 [l_i,u_i]=[\alpha_i^p-\beta_{i+1}^q,
                   \beta_{i+1}^p-\alpha_i^q].
\]
They overlap every output $g_m(t)$ on the cell.  With equal mesh $L/N$,
the same rational width identity for the two components gives
\[
 \frac LN\sum_i(u_i-l_i)\le\frac{LV}{N}+2L\theta,
\]
where
\[
 V=(d_0^{p,+}(b)-d_0^{p,-}(a))
      +(d_0^{q,+}(b)-d_0^{q,-}(a))
\]
is a computable nonnegative rational.  Take $LV/N\le\varepsilon/2$
and $\theta\le\varepsilon/(4L)$.  Common refinements and prefix
intersections again construct valid integral pairs.  Apply the two
convex secant inequalities and remove the finitely many interior widths
of $p,q$, together with the endpoint comparison widths for $f$.
The conclusion is exactly the same pair of boxed FTC inequalities.
This corollary, rather than scalar evaluation of $p-q$, supports the
trigonometric and other convex-difference constructions in the book.

\subsection{The reverse direction uses the same endpoint language}
Suppose $g$ is a valid nondecreasing evaluator.  Pointwise continuity at
an interior rational $x$ means: for every positive rational $\varepsilon$
there is a positive rational $\delta$ such that, whenever $|t-x|<\delta$,
\[
 g_n^-(t)\le g_m^+(x)+\varepsilon,\qquad
 g_m^-(x)\le g_n^+(t)+\varepsilon
 \quad\text{for all }n,m.
\]
Run the monotone integral algorithm with variable rational endpoint $x$
to construct pairs $F_n(x)$.  Ordered finite rectangle averages prove
the endpoint convexity inequalities for $F$.  To prove that $g$
differentiates $F$, fix $x,h,\varepsilon$, use continuity to bound the
oscillation of all cell outputs near $x$, and refine the two integral
outputs and the output at $x$ until their remaining widths are less
than the allotted fraction of $\varepsilon|h|$.  Finite additivity and
these width bounds give the residual-pair test.  The derivative constructor
therefore terminates and overlaps $g$ at all pairs of stages.

This is a sufficient construction, not a universal requirement that every
function be a difference of convex functions.  Later series and ODE
constructions must likewise establish endpoint inequalities with their
explicit finite errors.  Neither their proofs nor this theorem may
introduce a scalar merely by suppressing a stage index.
