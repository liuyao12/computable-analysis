\chapter{Integrals and the Fundamental Theorem}\label{ch:integrals}

An integral is a rational-box algorithm, not an ambient area supplied by
completeness:
\begin{verbatim}
partition -> certified cell ranges -> add rectangles -> nested shrinking boxes
\end{verbatim}
All subdivisions, crude or fast, are computations to be compared. Lebesgue
measure and unrestricted measurable functions are outside scope.

\section{Rectangles}\label{sec:integrals-ftc-contract}
For \(a=x_0<\cdots<x_m=b\), cell boxes \([L_k,U_k]\) give
\[
 \sum_k (x_{k+1}-x_k)L_k\leq\int_a^b f\leq
 \sum_k (x_{k+1}-x_k)U_k.
\]
Left, right, and trapezoid sums are finite interval identities.

\section{Validity and FTC}
A valid raw has ordered nested boxes whose widths eventually fit every positive
rational tolerance.  A Lipschitz or monotone/finite-turn certificate supplies
this data; the project does not assert that every classical continuous function
is integrable.

\section{Circle and sine}
For \(\sin(\pi x)\) on \([0,1/2]\), rational endpoints are exact and each
interior dyadic sample receives its own nested-radical box.
\begin{figure}[!htbp]\centering
\intervalSineIntegralStageAnimation
\caption{Dyadic cells and independently refined sine-value boxes.}
\label{fig:interval-sine-integral-stage}\end{figure}
The tangent chart gives a completed Riemann--Stieltjes route. The equal-dyadic
candidate is executable; its remaining theorem is the all-depth geometric
overlap with that chart, not a hidden completeness step.
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.SinPiIntegral.dyadicNestedRadicalLeftSum}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.SinPiIntegral.DyadicTangentWitnessFamily}
\leanok

For a new integral, provide an evaluator, cell ranges or a modulus, nesting
and shrinking, and an overlap with an independently useful computation.  An
FTC theorem additionally provides a primitive and finite telescope.
