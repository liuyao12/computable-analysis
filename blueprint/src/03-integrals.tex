in Chapter~\ref{ch:rational-circle-trigonometry} remain the geometric
algorithms.  Integral formulas enter only after those algorithms exist, as
theorems saying that a Riemann-sum algorithm computes the same raw real as an
endpoint expression or an inverse branch.

For raw functions \(f\) and \(F\), a definite integral identity over
\([a,b]\) consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a certified Riemann-sum construction for \(f\) on \([a,b]\);
  \item a validity proof for the endpoint-difference raw real \(F(b)-F(a)\);
  \item an equivalence proof, in the raw-real overlap sense, between the
  constructed integral and that endpoint difference.
\end{enumerate}
The interval version packages the same statement when \(f\) and \(F\) are
already certified as \code{FunctionOnInterval}s.  The
\code{DefiniteIdentityFor} variant is the same endpoint-difference interface
for newer domain-aware \code{ConstructionFor} algorithms.  It is stable under
replacing the construction by an equivalent integral computation, via
\code{transportConstruction}, and two endpoint identities for the same
integrand and primitive have equivalent integral raw reals.  The
\code{MonotoneDefiniteIdentityFor} variant records the same endpoint identity
when the integral side is explicitly supplied by a monotone construction, and
forgets back to \code{DefiniteIdentityFor}.  The
\code{GeneralDefiniteIdentityFor} variant records the endpoint identity for
the public piecewise-monotone integral \code{generalIntegralFor}; a one-piece
monotone endpoint identity promotes to it by \code{ofMonotone}.  It can also
be transported across equivalent general constructions, forgotten back to the
ordinary endpoint-identity interface, or built from an ordinary
\code{DefiniteIdentityFor} together with an equivalent general construction.
The endpoint-additivity, linearity, scalar, and order-transfer laws are
available at the ordinary, monotone, and general-integral levels.

The default finite mesh for ordinary definite integrals is static dyadic:
stage \(n\) uses the equal subdivision of \([a,b]\) into \(2^n\) intervals.
This is deliberately different from the dynamic subdivision in
\(\arctan_{\mathrm{geom}}\), where the next partition is chosen by repeatedly
refining the rational circle parameters already present in the sector
algorithm.  When an FTC proof uses this default mesh, the bridge to endpoint
identities is \code{definiteIdentity\_of\_staticDyadicEffectiveFTC}.
\end{definition}

\section{Nondecreasing integrands and piecewise monotone integrals}

The first integral theorem should be for nondecreasing functions on rational
intervals.  Their lower and upper Riemann sums are endpoint sums whose gap is
controlled by endpoint variation times the mesh size, so the finite proof is
especially transparent.  Nonincreasing functions are the signed/order-reversed
variant, and the general integral is then a finite sum over monotone pieces.

The basic laws needed for calculus are additivity on adjacent intervals,
rational scalar compatibility, linearity for pointwise sums on the common
domain, and order preservation for pointwise inequalities.  The last property
is the calculus input needed to turn the pointwise arctangent-kernel bounds in
Chapter~\ref{ch:infinite-series} into cell-integral inequalities.  Lean records

public validity theorem is \code{generalIntegralFor\_valid}.  Lean also names
\code{ExactCellOrderPreservation}, the finite rational-cell version of
``pointwise bounds integrate to cell bounds'' used by the arctangent-series
comparison.  On the FTC side,
Lean now also
has the raw telescoping identity
\[
  (F(b)-F(a))+(F(c)-F(b))\sim F(c)-F(a),
\]
both for total raw functions and for interval-certified primitives.  This is
the endpoint algebra behind adjacent-interval additivity whenever the integral
has already been identified with a primitive endpoint difference.  The
\code{DefiniteIdentityFor} and \code{MonotoneDefiniteIdentityFor} bridge
theorems now perform this transfer directly: endpoint telescoping implies
additivity for the corresponding integral raw reals, while the
\code{integral\_equiv\_add\_of\_endpoint\_add} orientation matches the
linearity target for pointwise sums.  The same transfer is available for
arbitrary rational scaling, using the signed raw-real scaling validity and
equivalence theorems.  Order also transfers through FTC endpoint identities:
once two integrals are identified with primitive endpoint differences,
\code{integral\_le\_of\_endpoint\_le} reduces integral order to order between
the endpoint differences.
\end{theorem}

\[

be the corresponding inscribed and circumscribed geometric sector
contributions.  Lean proves the local refinement identities saying that the
increment/decrement update is exactly the change in the two geometric sums
when \([p,r]\) is split at \(q\).  It proves the analogous monotone-kernel

sums decrease under midpoint refinement.  It also proves the finite
comparison inequalities
\[
  L_{p,r}\le G_{p,r},\qquad
  U_{p,r}\le R_{p,r},
  \qquad
  L_{p,r}\le U_{p,r},\qquad
  G_{p,r}\le R_{p,r}.
\]
The tangent-addition chart now has the corresponding finite transport layer.
For \(T_u(t)=(u+t)/(1-ut)\), the kernel and the endpoint rectangles satisfy
\[
  \frac{1}{1+T_u(t)^2}
  =
  \frac{(1-ut)^2}{(1+u^2)(1+t^2)},
\]
\[
  L_{T_u(p),T_u(r)}
   =L_{p,r}\frac{1-ur}{1-up},
  \qquad
  R_{T_u(p),T_u(r)}
   =R_{p,r}\frac{1-up}{1-ur}.
\]

lower rectangle is no larger than the source lower rectangle, while the
transported upper rectangle is no smaller.  Lean lifts these inequalities to
finite partitions: the transported target bracket contains the source bracket,
the transported endpoints remain ordered, and a midpoint-refinement partition
of \([0,x]\) becomes an explicit cover of
\([T_u(0),T_u(x)]\).  For \(0\le u<1\), positivity of all chart denominators

\begin{theorem}[Integral arctangent gives another pi computation]
\label{thm:arctan-integral-pi}
{\footnotesize\raggedright
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.PiProofs.piFromArctanIntegral_equiv_piCircleArea_of_geom_agreement}\par
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.PiProofs.piFromArctanIntegralFor_equiv_piCircleArea_of_effectiveFTC}\par
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.PiProofs.four_arctanIntegralRectangleForAtOne_equiv_piCircleArea}\par

Any certified integral construction for
\[
  \arctan_{\int}(1)=\int_0^1 \frac{\dd t}{1+t^2}
\]
that agrees with the geometric sector arctangent gives a new raw-real
representative of \(\pi\):
\[
  4\arctan_{\int}(1)\sim \pi_{\mathrm{area}}.
\]
Thus the remaining analytic work is the function-level arctangent-integral
comparison \code{ArctanIntegralGeomFunctionAgreement}.  Independently, the
concrete rectangle-sum schedule at \(1\) already gives a completed finite
integral-style pi route:
\[
  4\,\code{arctanIntegralRectangleForAtOne}\sim \pi_{\mathrm{area}}.
\]
Because the kernel is nonincreasing on \([0,1]\), the same schedule is also
available through the monotone-integral interface:
\[
  4\,\code{arctanIntegralRectangleMonotoneForAtOne}
    \sim \pi_{\mathrm{area}}.
\]
Equivalently, the unit-branch construction
\code{arctanIntegralRectangleUnitData} gives the table-facing raw real
\code{piFromArctanIntegralRectangleUnitAtOne} and theorem
\code{piFromArctanIntegralRectangleUnitAtOne\_equiv\_piCircleArea}.  The same
rectangle schedule is also packaged as a \code{DefiniteIdentityFor} certificate
\code{arctanGeomUnitRectangleDefiniteIdentity}; Lean proves the resulting pi
raw real has the same stage computations as
\code{piFromArctanIntegralRectangleUnitAtOne}.  The monotone-packaged version
\code{arctanGeomUnitRectangleMonotoneDefiniteIdentity} records the same
endpoint identity as a \code{MonotoneDefiniteIdentityFor}, and Lean checks
that forgetting monotonicity, or transporting the construction by equivalence,
lands back in the ordinary endpoint-identity interface.  The theorem
\code{piFromArctanIntegralFor\_equiv\_piCircleArea\_of\_monotoneDefiniteIdentityFor}
closes the pi equivalence directly from this monotone endpoint-identity
interface.  Promoting this one-piece monotone identity to
\code{GeneralDefiniteIdentityFor} gives the public \code{generalIntegralFor}
version \code{piFromArctanGeomUnitRectangleGeneralDefiniteIdentity}, again
with a completed equivalence to \(\pi_{\mathrm{area}}\).  More generally, an
ordinary \code{DefiniteIdentityFor} can now feed this public general-integral
pi route as soon as its construction is proved equivalent to a
\code{generalIntegralFor} construction.

The same pi benchmark can also be closed through calculus.  If a
definite-integral identity proves that the arctangent kernel integral over
\([0,1]\) is the endpoint difference of a primitive equivalent to
\code{arctanGeom(1)}, then
\code{piFromArctanIntegralFor\_equiv\_piCircleArea\_of\_definiteIdentityFor}
closes the pi equivalence for the domain-aware \code{ConstructionFor}
interface.  The more explicit
\code{piFromArctanIntegralFor\_equiv\_piCircleArea\_of\_effectiveFTC} version
uses an \code{EffectiveFTC} certificate, and
\code{piFromArctanIntegralFor\_equiv\_piCircleArea\_of\_staticDyadicEffectiveFTC}
is the same bridge specialized to the static dyadic mesh used by the ordinary
bounded-integral interface.  Both have endpoint-agreement variants that
package scheduled endpoint validity and equivalence to the canonical endpoint
difference, and stage-schedule variants that construct this agreement from an
explicit cofinal monotone endpoint precision schedule.  The
candidate-derivative, curvature, and convex certificate routes now have the
analogous direct pi bridges, endpoint-agreement variants, and stage-schedule
variants.  The
curvature route is the one suited to concave primitives such as arctangent on
the unit interval.  These are supporting bridge theorems for the

\section{Projective and Improper Test Integrals}

The first useful test, simpler than the Gaussian integral but still genuinely
projective, is
\[
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\dd x}{x^4+a x^2+1}.
\]
For \(a>-2\), the classical value is
\[
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\dd x}{x^4+a x^2+1}
    =\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{a+2}}.
\]
Thus when \(a=b^2-2\) with rational \(b>0\), the integral should give the
rational pi multiple \(\pi/b\).  The especially clean benchmark is \(a=-1\):
\[
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\dd x}{x^4-x^2+1}=\pi.
\]

The rational algebra behind the test integral is already formalized.  First,
the quartic denominator is reciprocal after clearing the projective factor:
\[
  x^4 Q_a(1/x)=Q_a(x).
\]
Consequently, after the usual nonzero-denominator hypotheses are supplied, the
positive half-line folded to the unit interval has density
\[
  \frac{1}{Q_a(x)}+\frac{1}{x^2Q_a(1/x)}
    =\frac{1+x^2}{Q_a(x)}.
\]
Second, for \(x\ne0\),
\[
  x^2\left(\left(x-\frac1x\right)^2+a+2\right)
    =x^4+a x^2+1,
\]
and
\[
  \left(1+\frac1{x^2}\right)x^2=1+x^2.
\]
Pairing the positive half-line point \(x\) with its reciprocal \(1/x\) gives
\[
  \int_0^\infty \frac{\dd x}{x^4+a x^2+1}
   = \frac12\int_0^\infty
      \frac{1+x^2}{x^4+a x^2+1}\,\dd x.
\]
With \(u=x-1/x\), the preceding identities convert the symmetrized density to
\(\dd u/(u^2+a+2)\), so the remaining formal work is the improper rational
integral bookkeeping over the full projective line:
\[
  \frac12\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\dd u}{u^2+a+2}.
\]
There is also a compact chart for the clean case that avoids carrying an
infinite endpoint through the finite algebra.  On \(-1<x<1\), put
\[
  u=\frac{x}{1-x^2},\qquad
  \frac{\dd u}{\dd x}=\frac{1+x^2}{(1-x^2)^2}.
\]
Lean now proves directly that its Cauchy pullback is the everywhere-defined rational
density
\[
  \frac{1+x^2}{x^4-x^2+1}
  =\frac{1+x^2}{(1-x^2)^2}
    \frac{1}{1+\bigl(x/(1-x^2)\bigr)^2}.
\]
The left side has the exact rational value \(2\) at each compact-chart
endpoint \(x=\pm1\).  Thus the remaining analytic construction can use a
finite interval with a removable-endpoint certificate, rather than treating a
tail limit as primitive.  It still must prove the projective
change-of-variables theorem and build the integral, so this is not yet a
completed scoreboard computation.  The compact density itself is already a
total rational-name interval function on every rational interval; its
interval-level regularity is proved below.
For the pi case \(a=-1\), Lean proves the stronger uniform separation
\[
  \frac34\le x^4-x^2+1,
\]
as well as nonnegativity of the compact density.  In particular it proves the
needed denominator positivity:
\[
  u^2+1>0,\qquad x^4-x^2+1>0.
\]
The finite-difference identity
\[
 f(x)-f(y)=
 \frac{(y^2-x^2)\bigl(x^2+y^2+x^2y^2-2\bigr)}
 {(x^4-x^2+1)(y^4-y^2+1)},
 \qquad f(x)=\frac{1+x^2}{x^4-x^2+1},
\]
is formalized as well.  On the compact interval its factors now give the
checked rational bound
\[
  |f(x)-f(y)|\le 8|x-y|\qquad(-1\le x,y\le1).
\]
Consequently the total compact-density evaluator has a literal rational
\(\varepsilon\)-\(\delta\) certificate on \([-1,1]\), with
\(\delta=\varepsilon/8\).  More strongly, it now has the checked
interval-regular evaluator required by the general calculus interfaces: for
an input interval \(I\), evaluate \(f\) at its rational midpoint \(m\) and
return
\[
  [f(m)-8\operatorname{width}(I),\ f(m)+8\operatorname{width}(I)].
\]
This interval contains every rational point-value over \(I\), has width
\(16\operatorname{width}(I)\), and therefore meets output precision
\(1/n\) when the input width is at most \(1/(16n)\).  Thus the density is
now a \code{ContinuousFunctionOnInterval}, with no appeal to topology or
completed reals.  The generic checked bridge from interval regularity to
rational \(\varepsilon\)-\(\delta\) continuity also recovers the stated
continuity certificate.  The projective change-of-variables theorem and the
actual integral construction remain unchecked.
Thus the full finite pullback identity for \(a=-1\) is now available without
extra denominator hypotheses, under only \(x\ne0\).  Lean also identifies
\[
  \frac{1}{u^2+1}
\]
with the existing arctangent kernel \(\code{ArctanGeometry.integralKernel}(u)\),
so the pi-case quartic test now lands directly in the same rational kernel used
by the verified rectangle arctangent construction.
\end{theorem}

The integral identity target for the geometric trigonometric functions on
\([a,b]\) is the pair of definite integral statements
\[
  \int_a^b \cos(t)\dd t
  \quad\hbox{equals}\quad
  \sin(b)-\sin(a),
\]
\[
  \int_a^b \sin(t)\dd t
  \quad\hbox{equals}\quad
  -\cos(b)+\cos(a).
\]
In Lean this is a structure, not yet an existence theorem: it records the
precise derivative, integral-construction, and endpoint-equivalence data that
must be supplied by the later geometric proof.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Inverse elementary integral identities]
\label{def:inverse-elementary-integral-identities}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.IntegralIdentities.InverseElementaryIntegralIdentities}
\uses{def:definite-integral-identity}
\leanok
The inverse trigonometric and logarithmic comparison targets are:
\[
  \int_0^x \frac{\dd t}{1+t^2}
  \quad\hbox{equals the chosen arctangent branch at }x,
\]
\[
  \int_0^x \frac{\dd t}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}
  \quad\hbox{equals the chosen arcsine branch at }x,
\]
and
\[
  \int_1^x \frac{\dd t}{t}
  \quad\hbox{equals the chosen logarithm branch at }x.
\]
These are branch-local statements.  They are not imported into the geometric
definition of trigonometric functions; they are used to prove that different
constructions agree.
\end{definition}

\section{Proof Order}

The intended formalization order is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item prove the geometric trigonometric algorithms valid and prove the
  exact identities from Chapter~\ref{ch:rational-circle-trigonometry};
  \item prove the derivative certificates for geometric sine and cosine from
  the circle construction;
  \item feed those certificates into the definite-integral identity package;
  \item prove arctangent, arcsine, and logarithm integral identities on their
  chosen branches;
  \item prove the Abelian inverse representations agree with the corresponding
  geometric or power-series algorithms.
\end{enumerate}

This keeps the definitions honest: geometry first, then integrals as
comparison theorems.
