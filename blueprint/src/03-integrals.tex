\chapter{Integral Computations}\label{ch:integrals}

An integral is a new interval computation obtained by adding finite
rectangles and controlling the error.  It is not defined through an
antiderivative.  This independence is what will make the fundamental theorem
an equivalence theorem rather than a convention.

\section{Partitions and cell enclosures}
A rational tagged partition is
\[
 a=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_N=b,\qquad
 \xi_i\in[x_i,x_{i+1}]\cap\Q.
\]
Its mesh is $\max_i(x_{i+1}-x_i)$.  If rational numbers $L_i,U_i$ bound all
values of $g$ in the $i$th cell, form
\[
 B_P(g)=\left[\sum_i L_i\Delta x_i,\ \sum_i U_i\Delta x_i\right].
\]
The width is $\sum_i(U_i-L_i)\Delta x_i$.  A common refinement shows that
any two sound cell-enclosure computations overlap: on each refined cell,
a value at a rational sample is bounded by both corresponding parent
ranges.  This argument uses only finitely many computed values and their
order comparisons.

\begin{definition}[An integral computation]\label{def:integral-computation}
A supplied sequence of partitions and sound cell enclosures is an integral
construction if its rectangle widths shrink to every positive rational
tolerance.  Finite-prefix intersections of the $B_P(g)$ make a valid
number computation, denoted $\int_a^b g$.
\end{definition}

Any two such constructions are equivalent, by their cross-overlap and
shrinking widths.  Changing tags, using unequal subdivisions, or refining
values faster than the mesh therefore does not change the integral.

\section{Continuous functions: rectangles with a modulus}
Suppose $g$ has the interval continuity certificate of
Chapter~\ref{ch:foundations}.  For a desired tolerance $\eta$, choose mesh
at most $\mu_g(\eta)$ and evaluate $g(\xi_i)$ in a box of width at most
$\eta$.  Enlarge each sample box by $\eta$ on both sides.  These are sound
cell ranges, and their integral width is at most
\[
 3\eta(b-a).
\]
This constructs the integral of every function for which that finite data
has been provided.  It does not derive the data from pointwise rational
continuity or from an abstract compactness theorem.

\section{Monotone functions: the total variation controls the error}
Let $g$ be a nondecreasing rational-input computation on $[a,b]$.  Neither
continuity nor a continuity modulus is assumed.  Its endpoint sums satisfy
\begin{align*}
 L_P&=\sum_i g(x_i)\Delta x_i,\\
 U_P&=\sum_i g(x_{i+1})\Delta x_i,\\
 U_P-L_P&\le\mesh(P)\bigl(g(b)-g(a)\bigr).
\end{align*}
The last inequality is finite telescoping.  Coarse endpoint computations
give a rational upper bound $V$ for $g(b)-g(a)$.  If every endpoint value
is evaluated to box width at most $\eta$, the rational upper--lower gap
is at most
\[
 \mesh(P)V+2\eta(b-a).
\]
Thus monotonicity gives an integral algorithm even for a derivative with
an undetected corner in its primitive.  Negation handles decreasing
functions; finite sums and differences of these constructions are also
integrable.  A finite partition into monotone pieces gives the same result.

\section{Finite rules for integrals}
The rules below follow from rectangles and common refinements, before any
FTC is used:
\[
 \int_a^c g=\int_a^b g+\int_b^c g,\qquad
 \int_a^b(\alpha g+\beta h)=\alpha\int_a^b g+\beta\int_a^b h.
\]
For reversed endpoints define $\int_b^a g=-\int_a^b g$.
If $|g|\le B$, then
\[
 \left|\int_u^v g\right|\le B|v-u|.
\]
If two integrands have a uniform difference bound $\varepsilon$, their
integrals differ by at most $(b-a)\varepsilon$.  These estimates are
proved first for every finite sum and then for the resulting boxes.

Products are admitted when their supplied cell ranges shrink in total
width.  For bounded factors with cell widths $w_i,z_i$ and absolute bounds
$B,C$, the product cell width is at most $Bw_i+Cz_i$.  This proves closure
under products for the integral constructions used here.

An endpoint given by a number computation is handled by rational
bracketing.  Moving either endpoint by at most $h$ changes the integral
by at most $Bh$.  This is the required evaluation rule for variable
endpoints; the bound and a domain enclosure are part of its data.

\section{Recognizing the earlier geometric computations}
\label{sec:geometry-as-integrals}
The cone and sphere volumes of Chapter~\ref{ch:circle-sphere} were
constructed before any of the definitions in this chapter.  Their finite
cylinder stacks now give, by comparison with the corresponding rectangle
sums,
\[
 V_{\mathrm{cone}}=\pi\left(\frac rH\right)^2\int_0^H z^2\,dz,
 \qquad
 V_R=\pi\int_{-R}^{R}(R^2-z^2)\,dz.
\]
These are equivalences between existing constructions.  In particular the
cone calculation already proves $\int_0^H z^2\,dz=H^3/3$, without
invoking an antiderivative.  The cap stacks likewise give
\[
 V_{\mathrm{cap}}=\pi\int_0^h(2Rt-t^2)\,dt.
\]
Later the FTC supplies different proofs and faster endpoint computations
of these same quantities.  The sphere's surface area remains the
independent facet-and-frustum computation: no volume slicing identity is
silently used as a definition of curved surface area.

\section{Accumulation computes a derivative locally}
\label{sec:accumulation-derivative}
Given an integral construction for $g$ on every subinterval, put
$F(x)=\int_a^x g$.  If a neighborhood of a rational $x$ has
$|g(t)-g(x)|\le\varepsilon$, finite sums give
\[
 |F(x+h)-F(x)-h g(x)|\le\varepsilon |h|
\]
whenever the segment from $x$ to $x+h$ stays in that neighborhood.  Thus
at a rational continuity point of $g$, the difference quotients of $F$
approximate $g(x)$ with the indicated error.

For a nondecreasing $g$, adjacent integral averages are ordered, so $F$
is convex.  Moreover
\[
 g(x-h)\le\frac{F(x)-F(x-h)}h\le g(x)
 \le\frac{F(x+h)-F(x)}h\le g(x+h).
\]
The next chapter will turn precisely this sort of enclosure into a
\emph{derivative computation}.  No unknown derivative value has to be
postulated.

\section{Changing the sampling coordinate}
For increasing, interval-continuous $\alpha$ on a rational parameter
interval, and interval-continuous $g$ on its image, form Stieltjes sums
\[
 \sum_i g(\alpha(\xi_i))
          \bigl(\alpha(t_{i+1})-\alpha(t_i)\bigr).
\]
A modulus for $g\circ\alpha$ makes upper--lower errors at most its cell
oscillation times the total increment $\alpha(v)-\alpha(u)$.  This is
another explicit integral construction.  Image endpoints can be computed
rather than rational: approximate the finitely many endpoints, using the
bounded-integrand endpoint estimate above.  Common refinements then show
that it computes $\int_{\alpha(u)}^{\alpha(v)}g$.

For sine, take $\alpha(t)=2A(t)/\pi$ on $[0,1]$ and
$g(\alpha(t))=2t/(1+t^2)$.  This compares the tangent-chart samples with
the equal-angle samples without first differentiating the chart.
