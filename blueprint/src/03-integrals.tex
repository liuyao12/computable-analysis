\section{Constructive Definite Integrals}

This chapter is the comparison layer.  The trigonometric functions constructed
in Chapter~\ref{ch:rational-circle-trigonometry} remain the geometric
algorithms.  Integral formulas enter only after those algorithms exist, as
theorems saying that a Riemann-sum algorithm computes the same raw real as an
endpoint expression or an inverse branch.

\section{Monotone integrands and convex FTC}

The first integral theorem should be for monotone functions on rational
intervals.  A monotone integrand has upper and lower Riemann sums whose gap is
controlled by endpoint variation times the mesh size, so the finite proof is
especially transparent.

\begin{theorem}[Monotone integral construction]
\label{thm:monotone-integral-construction}
\uses{def:constant-integral-construction,def:definite-integral-identity}
A function with certified monotonicity on \([a,b]\) has a valid rational
Riemann-sum integral.  The construction uses left and right sums, or equivalent
interval sums, and the width estimate is the finite monotone-variation bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Convex FTC]
\label{thm:convex-ftc}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.convexFTC}
\uses{thm:monotone-integral-construction}
\leanok
For a convex primitive, the derivative is monotone in the interval sense, so
the fundamental theorem of calculus reduces to the monotone integral
construction plus endpoint subtraction.  The current Lean theorem packages the
completed derivative-bound route: once convexity supplies the local derivative
bounds on rational partition cells, the integral raw real is equivalent to the
endpoint-difference raw real.
\end{theorem}

\[
  L_{p,r}=(r-p)\frac{1}{1+r^2},
  \qquad
  R_{p,r}=(r-p)\frac{1}{1+p^2}
\]
be the lower and upper rectangle contributions for the decreasing kernel
\(1/(1+u^2)\).  Let
\[
  G_{p,r}=
    \frac{(r-p)(1+pr)}{(1+p^2)(1+r^2)},
  \qquad
  U_{p,r}=\frac{r-p}{1+pr}
\]
be the corresponding inscribed and circumscribed geometric sector
contributions.  Lean proves the local refinement identities saying that the
increment/decrement update is exactly the change in the two geometric sums
when \([p,r]\) is split at \(q\).  It proves the analogous monotone-kernel
inequalities saying that lower rectangle sums increase and upper rectangle
sums decrease under midpoint refinement.  It also proves the finite
comparison inequalities
\[
  L_{p,r}\le G_{p,r},\qquad
  U_{p,r}\le R_{p,r},
  \qquad
  L_{p,r}\le U_{p,r},\qquad
  G_{p,r}\le R_{p,r}.
\]
Consequently, for every finite nonnegative ordered partition, the rectangle
sum interval contains the geometric sector-sum interval, and in particular the
two finite intervals overlap.  Lean also proves that every nonnegative
\code{arctanGeom} refinement stage has such a partition, and that the loop
lower and upper bounds are exactly the corresponding geometric sums.  Thus the
rectangle-sum interval contains, and overlaps, the actual \code{arctanGeom}
stage interval.  The rectangle-sum intervals are themselves ordered and nested
along the actual arctangent loop.

Lean now also has the global Farey-prefix version of the same finite
rectangle sums.  At a fixed Farey stage, the lower prefix up to \(x\) uses the
cells whose right endpoint is \(\le x\), while the upper prefix uses the cells
whose left endpoint is \(<x\), thereby including the first cell that crosses
\(x\).  The approximation to \(\int_a^b du/(1+u^2)\) is then defined as a
difference of two prefixes.  This makes finite adjacent additivity exact:
the theorem \code{fareyIntegralStageInterval\_add} proves that the stage
interval for \([a,c]\) is the interval sum of the stages for \([a,b]\) and
\([b,c]\).

For every rational \(x\) with \(0\le x\le 1\), Lean also proves the mesh
estimate needed for validity.  The sum of squared cell lengths is exactly
\(x^2/2^n\) after \(n\) midpoint refinements, hence is bounded by \(2^{-n}\),
and each rectangle gap is bounded by \(2(r-p)^2\).  Therefore the concrete
rectangle-sum raw real \code{arctanIntegralRectangleRaw x} is valid on the
unit branch.  Lean packages the same schedule as
\code{arctanIntegralRectangleFor x}, an \code{Integral.ConstructionFor} for
the kernel on \([0,x]\), and proves this domain-aware construction equivalent
to \code{arctanGeom x} throughout the rational unit branch.  The zero endpoint
uses a small degenerate-loop invariant, while the positive endpoint proof is
exactly the finite rectangle/geometric overlap.  This is the finite algebraic
core of the comparison \(\arctan_{\int}=\arctan_{\mathrm{geom}}\).  Lean also
wraps this construction as
\code{arctanIntegralRectangleRepresentation} and proves the function-level
overlap agreement \code{arctanIntegralRectangleFunctionAgreement}.  Lean now
also packages the same construction as the unit-branch integral arctangent
\code{arctanIntegralUnit}; the theorem
\code{arctanIntegralRectangleUnitFunctionAgreement} proves the expected
agreement on \(0\le x\le1\).  The remaining cleanup is to identify the older
nonnegative point-Riemann \code{arctanIntegral(1)} API with this
domain-aware schedule, or replace that older API throughout.
\end{theorem}

  \int_0^x \frac{\dd t}{1+t^2}
  \quad\hbox{equals the chosen arctangent branch at }x,

  \int_0^x \frac{\dd t}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}

\section{Proof Order}

The intended formalization order is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item prove the geometric trigonometric algorithms valid and prove the
  exact identities from Chapter~\ref{ch:rational-circle-trigonometry};
  \item prove the derivative certificates for geometric sine and cosine from
  the circle construction;
  \item feed those certificates into the definite-integral identity package;
  \item prove arctangent, arcsine, and logarithm integral identities on their
  chosen branches;
  \item prove the Abelian inverse representations agree with the corresponding
  geometric or power-series algorithms.
\end{enumerate}

This keeps the definitions honest: geometry first, then integrals as
comparison theorems.
