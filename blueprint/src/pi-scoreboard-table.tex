Definition-complete means a valid raw real; registry-complete means that the
corresponding raw presentation has a formal chain of \code{RealRaw.Equiv}
theorems to the preferred area presentation.  The table is a compact
release-style coverage suite, not a numerator for a calculus-completeness
percentage.  It deliberately names natural theorem families rather than
presenting a catalogue of rearranged equations for pi.  The checked registry
corollaries are formalized by \code{PiProofs.PiCoverageBridge} and its
\code{equivalent} theorem.
The broader named registry is pairwise interoperable through
\code{PiProofs.piPresentation\_equiv}; that library convenience theorem is not
an additional scoreboard row.

The suite asks whether a construction useful elsewhere in calculus agrees
with an independently constructed value of the same constant.  Thus a row is
evidence for a capability gate, rather than a claim that five successful pi
proofs make five equal-sized contributions to scientific or engineering
calculus.  In particular, a continuous Peano--Baker theorem and a proof that
the selected exponential satisfies \(e^x{}'=e^x\) are major advances even
though neither creates a new pi row.

There are eight such capability rows.  Machin's single formula is displayed
inside the arctangent/power-series row: it is a separately checked,
substantially faster computation, but it exercises the same series-to-
geometric bridge rather than inflating the readiness count.

The primary \code{PiProofs.PiPresentation} registry retains supplementary
checked evaluators as regression implementations: polygon area, the two
perimeter normalizations (available as \code{pi.circumferenceStabilized} and
\code{pi.circumferenceReboxed}), Nilakantha, and direct cross-fan
circumference views.  Machin is listed above as its single power-series
formula; its certified bound \(w_n\le20/2^n\) is recorded by
\code{PiProofs.piMachinRate}.  Its direct cross-fan view is \code{pi.circumference} (also
\code{pi.circumferenceFan}).  The abstract \code{piCertified} handle additionally stores the
supplementary \code{pi.dirichletBeta}, \code{pi.integrationByPartsMesh},
\code{pi.triangleLogSeries}, and \code{pi.squareStieltjes} representatives.
Here \code{pi.dirichletBeta} is the literal formula
\(\pi=4L(1,\chi_4)\), stagewise the Leibniz computation.  These are useful
executable comparisons, but not additional readiness cells: beta reuses the
alternating-series bridge and the remaining three refine the checked finite
integration-by-parts bridge.  The primary named view
\code{pi.integrationByParts} is the reciprocal-log formula itself; its rate
\(w_n\le52/2^n\) is certified by
\code{Logarithm.piTriangleLogReciprocalIntegralRate}.  In contrast,
\code{pi.squareSubstitution} retains the square-pullback endpoint and is a
separate coverage row: it invokes the finite \(t=x^2\) substitution theorem,
with rate \(w_n\le56/2^n\), certified by
\code{Logarithm.piTriangleLogSquareSubstitutionIntegralRate}.  None of these facts
yet transports the logarithm to the canonical inverse-exponential
presentation.

The original \code{piCircumference} evaluator is now valid.  Exact rational
checks cover stages \(1,2,4,8\); a fourth-order secant--curvature margin and a
dyadic exponent bound cover every later stage.  The resulting
\code{PiProofs.piCircumferenceDirect\_equiv\_piCircleArea} strengthens the
existing geometric row rather than adding a second scoreboard capability.
The checked finite integration-by-parts and square-substitution rows are
deliberately narrower than the future canonical formulation.  The former's
exact finite acceptance contract is stated in
\cref{def:integration-by-parts-certificate,thm:integration-by-parts-certificate}:
every monotone piece must provide its product box, common refinement,
corner-rectangle bound, and rational error schedule.  The concrete target is
then isolated in \cref{cor:arctangent-logarithm-pi-route}.  The two-sided product derivative
of the actual \(x\arctan x\) evaluator and its supplied finite FTC comparison
are checked; general monotone-piece composition remains the calculus gate.
The canonical refinement is deliberately the long elementary-function route:
its \(\log 2\) endpoint must agree with the inverse
of the canonical exponential.  The linear Peano--Baker/Picard--Lindel\"of
theorem is reserved for vector systems.  The scalar exponential
identification instead needs the direct finite-mesh contraction theorem, after
the individual presentations have supplied their own derivative and
initial-value certificates.  Thus the prospective refinement tests a
capability the checked finite row does not.
The Euler/complex-logarithm row above is a second, longer exp/log/ODE
corroboration; its rational complex factorial-tail raw is now checked, but it
remains unmarked pending rotation/geometric and branch certificates.  The
arcsine/Newton and Gaussian rows remain future probes for
inverse/square-root integration and the exponential/full-line layers; other
advanced routes are intentionally omitted from this progress table.
