\chapter{Computing Derivatives and the Fundamental Theorem}
\label{ch:effective-calculus}

The derivative should be constructed from the function.  For convex
functions, neighboring secants provide its lower and upper bounds.  We
prescribe the increments and evaluation stages, then prove that the
resulting pairs are ordered, nested, and shrinking.  An error estimate
justifies a computation; it need not direct that computation's execution.

\section{What pointwise rational differentiation misses}
For rational $x,h$, the expression $[f(x+h)-f(x)-hg(x)]_n$ means finite
interval arithmetic on the prescribed stage outputs.  To say that $g$
differentiates $f$ at $x$ is an endpoint-residual relation: for every
positive rational $\varepsilon$, all sufficiently small nonzero rational
$h$ admit a stage at which this residual pair is contained in
$[-\varepsilon|h|,\varepsilon|h|]$.  The witnessing stage in the proof
may depend on $h$.  The literal quantifiers appear in the endpoint-only
FTC section below.

Pointwise rational differentiation alone does not control a whole
interval.  The exact rational-pair evaluator
\[
 H_n(x)=\begin{cases}[0,0],&x^2<2,\\[1,1],&x^2>2\end{cases}
\]
is locally constant at every rational input, yet its endpoint-difference
output on $[0,2]$ is $[1,1]$.  The exact zero evaluator differentiates it
at every rational input.  Adding the geometric sine evaluator gives the
same failure with a nonconstant pointwise derivative.

This example has no interval-continuity certificate across the cut.
Convexity is a stronger finite interval condition which will make secants
into sound derivative brackets.  Unindexed expressions below are compact
relations between programs; the final FTC is stated entirely in endpoints.

\section{Convexity is a finite interval condition}
\begin{definition}
An evaluator $f$ is convex on a rational interval when
\[
 f_n^-((1-t)x+ty)\le(1-t)f_m^+(x)+t f_k^+(y)
\]
for rational $x,y$ in the interval, rational $0\le t\le1$, and all
stages $n,m,k$.  This does not require either endpoint function at a fixed
stage to be convex separately.
\end{definition}
Writing $s(u,v)$ for the program $(f(v)-f(u))/(v-u)$, the defining
inequalities imply the program-order comparisons
\[
 s(u,v)\le s(u,w)\le s(v,w)\qquad(u<v<w).
\]
They are proved by rearranging rational convexity inequalities at refined
stages and removing the finite endpoint-width slack.  Conversely, those
secant comparisons imply the displayed convexity condition.

On a smaller interval $[a,b]$ with rational outer points $A<a<b<B$,
\[
 s(A,a)\le s(x,y)\le s(b,B)\qquad(a\le x<y\le b).
\]
Coarse outputs of the two outer secants give a rational Lipschitz bound
in the endpoint sense of Chapter~\ref{ch:foundations}.  No real-variable
convexity theorem is used.

Midpoint convexity suffices for rational weights.  Pad a list of $n$
rational inputs with copies of its rational average to a list of length
$2^k$.  Repeated midpoint inequalities and finite cancellation give the
$n$-term Jensen inequality.  Repeated copies of two inputs give rational
weights.  With pair outputs, carry the finite widths through cancellation
and remove them by refinement, rather than cancel interval pairs as if
they were scalar values.

\section{The convex FTC}
\begin{theorem}[Fundamental theorem for a computed convex derivative]
\label{thm:convex-ftc}
Let $f$ be convex on a rational interval containing $[a,b]$ in its
interior.  At every rational $x\in[a,b]$, suppose the specified
fixed-schedule derivative construction has a proved shrinking-width bound.
Write $Df$ for this valid derivative program.  Every independent sound
shrinking rectangle construction for $Df$ is equivalent to the endpoint
subtraction program:
\[
 \boxed{\int_a^b Df(x)\,dx\ \sim\ f(b)-f(a).}
\]
A monotone rectangle schedule with the required properties is described
in the endpoint-only section below.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Secant ordering and the endpoint derivative relation give
\[
 (v-u)[Df(u)]_k^-\le f_m^+(v)-f_m^-(u),\qquad
 f_m^-(v)-f_m^+(u)\le(v-u)[Df(v)]_k^+
\]
for every $m,k$ and rational $u<v$.  They also give the endpoint
monotonicity of $Df$.  Finite endpoint sums thus bound every primitive
endpoint-difference output, after removing the interior evaluation widths.
Their mesh error is bounded by the mesh times a rational bound for the
endpoint increase of $Df$; sample widths give a separate finite error.
Common refinements prove integral validity without reading $f$.
The detailed endpoint proof below then identifies the two algorithms.
\end{proof}

The derivative's stage rule stays fixed during integration.  The integral
plan must specify which derivative stages it samples and prove that the
resulting finite sum of widths shrinks.  This is automatic from explicit
rates in the concrete examples, not from replacing a stage by an unstated
precision search.  One-sided or improper endpoint variants require their
own domain and endpoint-tail estimates.

\begin{example}[An irrational corner is allowed]
For the exact rational-pair evaluator of $\max(0,x^2-2)$, a fixed sequence
$h_n=h_0/(n+1)$ eventually stays on one polynomial branch at each rational
input.  Its derivative construction is therefore valid there and agrees
with the exact pair $[0,0]$ when $x^2<2$, and $[2x,2x]$ when $x^2>2$.
On $[0,2]$ the derivative is monotone but not uniformly continuous.
The latter exact evaluator has no sample-width error, so equal $N$-cell
endpoint sums have gap $8/N$ and the integral is equivalent to $[2,2]$.
A hidden corner is allowed; an unrecorded jump in the primitive is not.
\end{example}

\section{Differences of convex computations}
If $f$ agrees with $u-v$, where the two convex computations have valid
fixed-schedule derivatives, define the derivative program by $Du-Dv$.
The endpoint derivative relation proves independence of the chosen
successful construction, and linearity of integral algorithms gives the
same endpoint FTC.  Concave functions are included by negation, and
compatible finite pieces by splitting and matching their endpoint programs.

A convenient certificate is convexity of $f+Kx^2$ for a rational $K\ge0$.
It must be proved by finite inequalities or remainder estimates.  A lower
bound on a pointwise rational second derivative alone is insufficient.

\subsection{Polynomials need only the binomial identity}
For $P(x)=\sum_{n=0}^d a_nx^n$ on $[-R,R]$, $R\ge1$ rational, finite
binomial algebra gives
\[
 |P(x+h)-2P(x)+P(x-h)|\le2K h^2,
 \qquad K=\sum_{n=2}^d|a_n|2^{n-1}R^{n-2},
\]
when $x\pm h$ remain in the interval.  Here every polynomial expression
is rational.  Thus $P+Kx^2$ is convex, and its exact-pair secant gaps are
at most $4K|h|$.  Any fixed $h_n\to0$ works, without a source-width
condition because these polynomial outputs are exact.
The centered quotient identifies the derivative with
$\sum_{n=1}^d n a_nx^{n-1}$.  Applied to $x^{n+1}/(n+1)$, the FTC gives
\[
 \int_a^b x^n\,dx\sim
 \left[\frac{b^{n+1}-a^{n+1}}{n+1},
       \frac{b^{n+1}-a^{n+1}}{n+1}\right]_{m\in\N}.
\]
A larger interval supplies endpoint neighborhoods.

\section{The geometric sine and its normalization}
Put $U=S+8x^2$, an expression for a composite evaluator.  The geometric
addition and chord comparisons of Chapter~\ref{ch:rational-circle-trigonometry}
give, as program-order inequalities,
\[
 0\le U(x+h)-2U(x)+U(x-h)\le32h^2.
\]
They follow by substituting
$16h^2-2S(x)(1-C(h))$ and $0\le1-C(h)\le8h^2$; each inequality is a
cross-stage endpoint statement.  Thus $U$ is convex.  For a fixed source
schedule the actual derivative bracket has width at most
$32h_n+\rho_n(x)$.  A source-resolution theorem $\rho_n(x)\to0$ is
therefore required for that schedule.  It is not supplied by pointwise
validity alone.

The addition identity identifies the centered secant program after the
quadratic subtraction with $C(x)S(h)/h$.  The geometric small-angle
comparison $|S(h)-\pi h|\le32|h|^3$, interpreted at endpoints with
removable rational slack, identifies its derivative as $\pi C$.
The corresponding cosine calculation gives $DC\sim-\pi S$.
These are program comparisons; products involving $\pi$ include the
prescribed stages of that constant's pair evaluator.

Once valid sampling and derivative schedules have been provided, the FTC
therefore gives the familiar mnemonic formulas
\[
 \int_a^b S(x)\,dx\sim\frac{C(a)-C(b)}\pi,\qquad
 \int_a^b C(x)\,dx\sim\frac{S(b)-S(a)}\pi.
\]
The quotient signs denote separated reciprocal computations.  On $[0,1/2]$
the first comparison is with the reciprocal-$\pi$ program.  This geometric
identification is separate from the fixed-schedule factorial representation
worked out above; a comparison of representations must be proved rather
than supplied by their common name.

\section{Products and integration by parts}
For differences of convex computations with valid derivative programs,
let $V_f$ bound the sum of the endpoint increases of their monotone
derivatives.  The finite FTC argument bounds the total increment-replacement
error by $\mesh(P)V_f$, with additional explicitly accounted sample widths.
Let $L_f,B_f$ be rational Lipschitz and absolute bounds.
For two such computations the finite product identity gives
\[
 \Delta(fg)=f(x_i)\Delta g+g(x_i)\Delta f+\Delta f\,\Delta g.
\]
It is first an identity of rational sample expressions; transporting it
to arbitrary interval outputs introduces their widths, which are retained
and then removed.  The accumulated error bound is
\[
 \mesh(P)\bigl(B_fV_g+B_gV_f+L_fL_g(b-a)\bigr)
\]
plus the finite sample-width budget.  Product cell ranges are integrable
by the rectangle estimate in the preceding chapter.  Consequently
\[
 \int_a^b f\,Dg\sim f(b)g(b)-f(a)g(a)-\int_a^b g\,Df.
\]
This neither selects a mean-value point nor assumes a convex decomposition
of the product in advance.

\section{Substitution and further integral identities}
Suppose $\alpha$ is increasing, is a difference of convex computations
with valid derivatives, and $g$ has interval continuity on its image.
The Stieltjes sums have terms $g(\alpha(x_i))\Delta\alpha_i$.
Replacing increments by derivative samples changes the sum by at most
$B_g\mesh(P)V_\alpha$, with their finite sample widths added.  Hence
\[
 \int_{\alpha(a)}^{\alpha(b)}g(u)\,du
 \sim\int_a^b g(\alpha(t))D\alpha(t)\,dt.
\]
Reversing orientation and splitting finitely many monotone pieces gives
the other versions.  Every substitution retains its domain, endpoint,
and increment estimates.  A formal chain rule at rational points is not
an interval FTC certificate.

For the angle-area program, accumulation gives the derivative evaluator
$[1/(1+t^2),1/(1+t^2)]$.  Ordered integral averages prove concavity.
Its explicit continuity bound supplies the secant convergence estimate;
the chosen source schedule must also meet the resolution condition.
Product and substitution identities then apply to this same computation.
For example
\[
 \int_0^1\frac{t\,dt}{1+t^2}\sim\frac12\int_1^2\frac{du}{u},
\]
which motivates the logarithm construction.
