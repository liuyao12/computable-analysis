We study the space of computations—slow rectangles and fast series—\nand the relations between them.\n\n\section{Finite stages and Fourier data}\n\begin{theorem}[Finite Fourier orthogonality]\label{thm:finite-fourier-orthogonality}\nFinite rational samples verify orthogonality and Parseval identities.  They\nare stages of a potential-infinity transform, not a completed Fourier theory.\n\end{theorem}\n\begin{theorem}[Prime reciprocal stages]\label{thm:finite-prime-reciprocal-extension}\nEach certified prime adds a positive rational term; the accumulator and its\nprecision are checked.  Divergence is a later target.\n\end{theorem}\n\label{sec:series-benchmark-foundations}\label{thm:arithmetic-series-sum}\n\label{thm:finite-power-sums}\label{thm:geometric-series-sum}\n\label{thm:reciprocal-triangular-sum}\label{thm:harmonic-dyadic-lower-bound}\n\n\section{Pi: exploring computations}\n\nArchimedes' subdivisions, binomial expansions, termwise integration, and\nMachin identities are different routes through the same computation space.\nThis records a general pattern, not a claim about Newton's motives.\n\begin{verbatim}\nstage(n) := finite_sum(first n terms)\ntail(n)  := explicit rational enclosure\nreturn stage(n) +/- tail(n)\n\end{verbatim}\n\label{def:alternating-series-intervals}\label{thm:nilakantha-pi}\n\label{thm:power-series-arctan-area-pi}\label{thm:leibniz-arctan}\n\n\section{Power series}\n\nRepresent coefficients and derivative data rationally; compose only when a\nmajorant gives a computable tail.
