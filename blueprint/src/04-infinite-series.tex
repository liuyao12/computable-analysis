\chapter{Power Series and Fourier Analysis}\label{ch:infinite-series}

An infinite series is represented by a finite prefix and a certified rational
tail.  The tail, not a completed sum, is the analytic input.

\begin{verbatim}
requested precision -> choose finite stage -> compute prefix
                    -> attach tail box -> realRaw or complex raw value
\end{verbatim}

The scoped Lean entry point is
\texttt{ComputableAnalysis.SeriesFoundation}.  Finite algebra is reused from
Lean; this chapter records only the coefficient identities and tail mechanisms
that lead to genuinely infinite processes.

\section{Finite Taylor algebra}

Differentiation and integration begin as coefficient shifts.  For a finite
prefix, integrating term by term is exact rational algebra: divide the
coefficient of \(x^n\) by \(n+1\), and the endpoint difference is the finite
sum of the monomial integrals.  No interchange with an infinite sum has yet
occurred.

\section{Pi and power series}
\label{thm:shared-rational-anchor}
\label{thm:leibniz-arctan}

The project studies the space of computations, fast and slow alike.  Crude
rectangles, binomial expansions, termwise integration, and alternating
prefixes are compared through finite overlap certificates.  This is a general
lesson about discovery, not a claim about Newton's private reasoning:
breakthroughs often come from understanding neighboring computations rather
than directly optimizing the original one.

\begin{verbatim}
choose finite route -> compute enclosure -> compare with shared anchor
                    -> refine only the needed certificate
\end{verbatim}

For the arctangent kernel, the integrated Taylor prefix and the Leibniz prefix
are the same finite rational computation.  The alternating remainder gives
the potential-infinity budget; comparison with the geometric quarter-turn is
a separate representation edge.

\section{Fourier reconstruction frontier}

A foundational Fourier-series result should not merely construct each
coefficient.  It must give a nontrivial infinite family and a uniform
algorithm for reconstruction error:

\begin{verbatim}
input evaluator -> finite coefficients -> finite synthesis
                -> uniform tail bound -> pointwise or energy reconstruction
\end{verbatim}

Finite transforms, stabilization, and geometric-tail examples are complete.
The missing major theorem is effective reconstruction for a genuinely
infinite trigonometric family, followed by termwise integration and
differentiation under explicit tail schedules.

\section{Basel and factorial growth}
\label{thm:basel-geometric-overlap}

The computable Basel target compares two finite-enclosure algorithms:
reciprocal-square prefixes with a telescoping tail, and an independent
geometric or Fourier area computation.  The final theorem is their maintained
overlap edge.  The necessary finite tail algebra is available, but the
independent geometric identification remains a frontier.

Factorial growth is treated in the same style.  Sine, exponential, Gaussian,
and ODE computations use finite factorial-tail searches rather than a global
theory of asymptotics.

\section{Interface}

\begin{verbatim}
finite coefficient identity -> prefix evaluator -> explicit tail
                            -> stabilized raw value -> equivalence edge
\end{verbatim}

Every power-series or Fourier theorem should enter through this chain.  A
routine sign, scaling, finite-support, or finite-algebra variant is transported
from an existing representative rather than becoming another foundation
theorem.
