\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

The aim of this project is to formalize a strain of classical mathematics
that was already effective in practice before the modern completeness theory
of the real numbers was isolated.  The guiding idea is simple: replace an
appeal to a completed continuum by a transparent rational interval algorithm,
then prove the finite estimates that make the algorithm valid.

The main objects are therefore not arbitrary points of a complete metric
space.  They are algorithms
\[
  n \longmapsto [a_n,b_n],
  \qquad a_n,b_n\in\Q,\qquad a_n\le b_n,
\]
whose intervals are nested and whose widths shrink to zero.  Equality means
overlap at every requested precision, possibly after cofinal changes of
stage.  This is strong enough for the fundamental theorem of calculus, the
fundamental theorem of algebra, Archimedes' computation of pi, Euler's
series arguments, and the elementary functions, but it keeps the proof
obligations finite and inspectable.
