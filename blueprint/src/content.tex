\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

The aim of this project is to formalize a strain of classical mathematics
that was already effective in practice before the modern completeness theory
of the real numbers was isolated.  The guiding idea is simple: replace an
appeal to a completed continuum by a transparent rational interval algorithm,
then prove the finite estimates that make the algorithm valid.

The main objects are therefore not arbitrary points of a complete metric
space.  They are algorithms
\[
  n \longmapsto [a_n,b_n],
  \qquad a_n,b_n\in\Q,\qquad a_n\le b_n,
\]
whose intervals are nested and whose widths shrink to zero.  Equality means
overlap at every requested precision, possibly after cofinal changes of
stage.  This is strong enough for the fundamental theorem of calculus, the
fundamental theorem of algebra, Archimedes' computation of pi, Euler's
series arguments, and the elementary functions, but it keeps the proof
obligations finite and inspectable.

\section*{Scoped benchmark theorem index}
\label{sec:scoped-benchmark-index}
The current scope contains 39 checked cores from the 100-theorem benchmark.
The links below go directly to the relevant chapter statements or to the
benchmark subsections that collect their Lean declarations.  They are cores
in the project's rational, finite, or certificate language; a link does not
claim the unrestricted classical theorem when the chapter marks that bridge
as deferred.

\begin{description}
\item[Foundations.] \ref{rem:algebraic-numbers-sqrt-two} (irrationality
  of \(\sqrt2\)); \ref{thm:rational-code-surjection} (coding of
  \(\mathbb Q\)); \ref{sec:benchmark-foundations} (38, 52, 58, 60, 69,
  74, 78, 80, 85, 91} (AM--GM, counting, Bézout/gcd, induction,
  Cauchy--Schwarz, factor certificates, divisibility, and triangle
  inequalities).

\item[Circle and trigonometry.] \ref{thm:rational-circle-pythagorean}
  (Pythagorean and parametrization cores); \ref{thm:geometric-pi-validity}
  (circle area); \ref{thm:rational-circle-group-law} (finite
  de Moivre-style circle law); \ref{sec:benchmark-geometry} (27, 55, 57,
  65, 95) (triangle orientation, chord power, Heron, isosceles geometry,
  and Ptolemy); \ref{thm:law-of-cosines-equilateral} (law of
  cosines).

\item[Infinite series.] \ref{thm:zeta-two-valid} (Basel-series
  interval/tail core); \ref{thm:leibniz-arctan} (Leibniz series);
  \ref{thm:harmonic-dyadic-lower-bound} (harmonic growth);
  \ref{sec:series-benchmark-foundations} (42, 44, 66, 68) (telescoping,
  binomial, geometric, and arithmetic finite sums).

\item[Effective calculus.] \ref{thm:effective-ftc-certificate}
  (FTC endpoint certificate); \ref{thm:finite-monomial-secants}
  (finite Taylor core); \ref{thm:cubic-secants} (quotient
  cancellation); \ref{thm:square-midpoint-mean-value} (finite MVT
  core); \ref{thm:constructive-inverse-function} (constructive
  intermediate-value search).

\item[Algebra and FTA.] \ref{thm:linear-factor-remainder-base} (37, 46,
  89, 100) (cubic and quartic worked cores, factor/remainder certificates,
  and sign-variation data).

\item[Differential equations.] \ref{thm:two-by-two-cayley-hamilton}
  (finite Cayley--Hamilton core); \ref{thm:cramer-two-by-two}
  (two-by-two Cramer rule).
\end{description}
