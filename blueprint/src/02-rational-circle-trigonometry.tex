real trigonometry.  Exact rational coordinates give squared identities;
\texttt{realRaw} length or angle computations are added only where the
application needs them.

\section{Archimedes and rational circles}
\label{thm:geometric-pi-validity}

Crude subdivisions are not failed approximations.  They are finite
computations whose inclusion and refinement relations can be proved.

\begin{verbatim}
rational circle points -> inscribed/circumscribed cells
                       -> finite area and length bounds -> refine
\end{verbatim}

The resulting area and circumference computations are independent
representations.  Their equivalence is a theorem in the \(\pi\) scoreboard,
not a definitional identification.

\section{Angles and rotations}
\label{thm:finite-quarter-turn}

The blueprint writes sine as \(\sin(\pi x)\).  On the half-period, the circle
algorithm may internally use the normalized parameter \(t=2x\); this is an
implementation coordinate, not a second sine convention.

The half-angle computation is the central algorithm:

\begin{verbatim}
parent cosine box -> (1 +/- cosine)/2 -> rational square-root box
                  -> child sine/cosine boxes -> repeat
\end{verbatim}

Every finite stage carries the unit-circle identity, endpoint values, and
refinement law.  Changing internal precision gives overlapping boxes, so
later proofs may change schedules through an explicit representation edge.
The table's oriented cosine is folded to its nonnegative magnitude before the
half-angle quotient is formed.  The resulting rational tangent boxes shrink,
overlap across precisions, and stabilize to a valid computation without a
completed-real anchor.

\section{Input to integration}

This chapter supplies value boxes.  Chapter~\ref{ch:integrals} supplies the
area and FTC certificates.  Its first target,
\[
  \int_0^{1/2}\sin(\pi x)\,dx,
\]
uses either equal dyadic nested-radical samples or unequal samples coming from
uniform tangent subdivisions.

\begin{verbatim}
circle/arctangent geometry -> rational sine/cosine value boxes
                           -> rectangle or Stieltjes integral
\end{verbatim}

The remaining equal-dyadic obligation is geometric: positive odd
nested-radical samples must overlap the corresponding arctangent-backed circle
boxes at every depth.  Boundary, parity, reflection, finite-search soundness,
and integral transport are handled in the integral chapter.

\section{Scope boundary}

Only geometry that certifies an infinite process is developed here.  The
natural dense chart may be rational coordinates, dyadic angle samples, or a
finite algebraic extension; it need not be a formula on rational numbers
alone.  Overlapping charts are connected by explicit equivalence edges.
