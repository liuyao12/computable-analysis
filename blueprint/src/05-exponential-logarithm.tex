\chapter{Exponential and Logarithm}\label{ch:exponential-logarithm}

Power series and the effective FTC now provide the two basic tools: evaluate
finite prefixes with tails, and transport derivative identities to endpoint
identities. Exponential, logarithm, and their trigonometric connections are
built from these tools.

\section{Exponential}

\begin{verbatim}
Taylor prefix -> remainder bound -> rational enclosure -> refine
\end{verbatim}

The exponential is introduced by its rational Taylor evaluator.  Its algebra
and differential laws are finite prefix identities plus transported tail
bounds; no completed real line is needed.
\begin{verbatim}
expPrefix(x,N) -> expTailBound(x,N) -> expInterval(x,eps)
                 -> exp(x+y), derivative, inverse certificates
\end{verbatim}
The certified power-series evaluator is exposed independently of domain
proofs, with geometric tail metadata. The reciprocal kernel is likewise
exposed on the positive interval \([1,2]\); its interval image is
\([1/I_{\rm hi},1/I_{\rm lo}]\).

The evaluator contract is uniform across rational inputs:

\begin{verbatim}
input x -> choose N from the tail budget
        -> sum x^k/k! for k <= N
        -> enclose the signed remainder
        -> return a rational interval
\end{verbatim}

The tail argument is itself finite.  After choosing the first omitted degree,
every later finite Taylor prefix is enclosed by the same rational factorial
majorant.  This is the project’s potential-infinity replacement for asserting
that a sequence has converged in a completed real line.

Algebraic laws are proved by comparing two such finite computations. Identity
of two expressions is an overlap theorem between their raw intervals, not an
appeal to an ambient completed field.

the rotation coordinates. Their effective derivative laws are checked:

\begin{verbatim}
exp(i*x) -> (cos(x), sin(x))
sin' = cos,    cos' = -sin
\end{verbatim}

This is the computable Euler bridge; no completed complex or real continuum is
needed.

\section{Logarithm and Euler identity}

Reciprocal bounds and finite composition provide the computable core. The
completed logarithm bridge is recorded only when its representation is made
explicit.

On a positive rational interval, logarithm can be represented by a monotone
inverse-exponential search.  The concrete branch \(\log 2\) is already
formalized in two equivalent ways: an alternating reciprocal series and a
dyadic integral of \(1/x\) on \([1,2]\).  Their equivalence is proved by
finite rational box comparisons and is used in the arctangent/\(\pi\) bridge.
The remaining transport is specifically the identification of this branch
with the canonical inverse of the exponential evaluator.
\begin{verbatim}
positive target -> monotone inverse search -> log interval
log/exp overlap -> log identities and Euler rotation law
\end{verbatim}

The inverse search follows the same pattern as the tangent search in the
integral chapter: a rational source cell is bisected, the forward exponential
image is enclosed, and a finite separation certificate selects the next cell.
Thus logarithm requires no global inverse-function theorem; each positive
interval branch carries its own source, range, and separation data.

The separation certificate is explicit for the unit exponential: positive
finite Taylor coefficients give the linear lower bound
\[
  y-x \le \operatorname{expCenter}(y)-\operatorname{expCenter}(x),
\]
and a gap-dependent factorial stage makes the endpoint boxes disjoint.
