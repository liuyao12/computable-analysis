\chapter{Exponential and Logarithm}
\label{ch:exponential-logarithm}

This chapter keeps the three computational routes separate until Lean proves
their raw interval algorithms equivalent.  The intended long route is
\[
 \text{power series / repeated multiplication / inverse log}
 \longrightarrow E'=E,\ E(0)=1
 \longrightarrow \text{direct scalar mesh uniqueness}
 \longrightarrow \log=E^{-1}.
\]
It uses rational boxes and tail bounds throughout; it never asks for a
complete ambient real line.

rational lower bound.  For every such base \(b\), define
\[
 b^0=1,\qquad b^{n+1}=b^n b.
\]
The code proves validity and positivity of every natural power by interval
multiplication.  This is the only meaning of ``repeated multiplication'' used
in the foundation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Rational powers]
\label{def:rational-powers}
\lean{ComputableAnalysis.exp.RationalPowerExtension}
A rational-power extension supplies valid positive boxes \(b^q\), agrees with
the preceding recursion at \(q\in\mathbb N\), and certifies
\[
 b^{p+q}=b^p b^q,\qquad (b^{p/q})^q=b^p\quad(q>0).
\]
It is an interface: root construction is a separate algorithm, not an appeal
to an unrepresented real number.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The deliberately long ODE route]
\label{rem:ode-exp-log-pi-route}
The common-prefix factorial representation has now proved
\(E'=E,E(0)=1\) on its unit chart.  Once the other exponential
representations have corresponding certificates and constructive linear ODE
uniqueness is proved, those representations are identified.  For scalar
exponential this is a direct finite-mesh proof: a short rational block
repeatedly halves the error envelope, without Peano--Baker or Picard
iteration.  Their positive inverse becomes the canonical logarithm; the
preceding endpoint transport then renames the integration-by-parts endpoint.
This is a dependency route, not a ninth \(\pi\) scoreboard cell.
\end{remark}
