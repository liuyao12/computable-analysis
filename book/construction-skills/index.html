An elementary foundation for integration

Every proposed integration theory should recover piecewise-constant functions on intervals and continuous piecewise-linear paths. The finite values are

\[I_a^b(s)\simeq\sum_i c_i(x_{i+1}-x_i),\qquad I_\gamma(s)\simeq\sum_i c_i(z_{i+1}-z_i).\]

Prove refinement, orientation, linearity, addition, and independence of finitely many breakpoint values. This common baseline does not require every other integrand to use the same approximation method. Existence for each function and uniqueness from justified comparisons remain separate tasks.

Particular functions as examples

The real-integral guide includes rational functions, trigonometric functions, powers and improper endpoints, and Gaussian and Gamma integrals. The complex guide covers geometry, path laws, and branches; the series guide covers ordered partial sums, tails, comparisons, and divergence.

The holomorphic-function guide compares several ways to construct derivative evidence. The continuation guide covers Taylor chains, functional equations, reflection, roots, and local ODE solutions. Their chapter examples distinguish zeros, singularities, and monodromy.
