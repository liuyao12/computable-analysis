Zeta for real exponents

The defining series now gives a certified computation for every represented real \(s>1\), using finite rational boxes.

Beyond integer inputs

The earlier zeta evaluator accepted integer exponents. The new function accepts any valid interval algorithm whose output eventually lies strictly above one. It finds a separating box, selects a rational interval on which the estimates hold, and refines the input as far as each output precision requires.

Equivalent input algorithms give equivalent answers, even if they select different intervals. At every integer \(n\ge2\), the result agrees with the earlier Dirichlet-series computation. In particular, the target \(\zeta(3)\) in the Apéry development is unchanged. The Basel case is now proved below; Euler’s general even-value formula remains open.

A power series inside a Dirichlet series

For a rational exponent \(s\), start with the generalized binomial coefficients:

\[ c_0(s)=1,\qquad c_{k+1}(s)=\frac{k+2-s}{k+1}\,c_k(s). \] \[ f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_k(s)z^k=(1-z)^{s-2}. \]

The checked recurrence is precisely the coefficient equation for \((1-z)f'=(2-s)f\) with \(f(0)=1\), and it has a unique formal coefficient solution. Substituting \(z=1-1/n\) gives the binomial computation of \(n^{-s}\).

Both infinite sums are handled through finite rectangles. With \(n\) starting at one, define

\[ D_k(N)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n^2}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^k, \] \[ R(s,K,N)=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}c_k(s)D_k(N). \]

Lean checks the exact interchange of these finite sums. Each rectangle is a finite Dirichlet sum with every power evaluated to the same binomial degree.

Two errors, both bounded

Work on \(1+1/(q+1)\le s\le m+2\), with \(q>0\). Put \(J=m+1\) and \(B=(m+3)^J\). The finite estimates are:

QuantityChecked bound
Moment\((k+1)D_k(N)\le2\)
Additional binomial degrees\(\lvert R(s,K+L,N)-R(s,K,N)\rvert\le2(q+1)\lvert c_K(s)\rvert\), for \(K\ge J\)
Additional Dirichlet terms\(\lvert R(s,K,N+L)-R(s,K,N)\rvert\le KB/N\), for \(N>0\)
Coefficient decay\(\displaystyle\lvert c_{J2^j}(s)\rvert\le B\left(\frac{2q+2}{2q+3}\right)^j\)

These estimates prove that the cutoff searches terminate. Intersecting the successively widened approximation boxes gives nested, valid output boxes. Explicit bounds on the finite polynomials’ variation then connect the construction to arbitrary represented real inputs through the existing adaptive function-application algorithm.

The convergence theorem controls the finite Dirichlet sums themselves: for any positive rational \(\varepsilon\), sufficiently many terms and sufficiently fine stages place every point in the finite-sum and zeta output boxes within \(\varepsilon\).

Basel and infinitely many primes

Basel is proved for our geometric \(\pi\) and the real-domain zeta function:

\[\zeta(2)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac1{k^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.\]

The proof uses finite rational sums and the previously checked Leibniz formula. Write \(B_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(-1)^k/(2k+1)\), \(H_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}1/(2k+1)\), and \(M_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}1/(2k+1)^2\). Diagonal and triangular rearrangements of a finite square give

\[\left|2B_N^2-M_N\right|\le\frac{2H_N}{N}\qquad(N\ge1).\]

The odd/even split then yields an explicit schedule:

\[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{2m^2}\frac1{k^2}-\frac83 B_{2m^2}^{\,2}\right|\le\frac{16}{m}\qquad(m\ge1).\]

These are checked finite inequalities. They establish equivalence of the existing interval computations; no completed real line or new complex analysis is introduced. The route is a finite reconstruction of Benko and Molokach’s rearrangement proof.

The number-theoretic application

For a finite list \(P\) of distinct primes, put \(c_P=\prod_{p\in P}(1-p^{-2})\), and let \(S_P(N)\) sum \(k^{-2}\) over the integers \(1\le k\le N\) divisible by none of those primes. The formalization proves

\[S_{\{p\}\cup P}(N)=S_P(N)-p^{-2}S_P(\lfloor N/p\rfloor),\]

and an explicit bound \(\lvert S_P(N)-c_P\sum_{k=1}^N k^{-2}\rvert\le K_P/(N+1)\), with \(K_{\varnothing}=0\) and \(K_{\{p\}\cup P}=(p+1)K_P+2p\). If \(P\) contained every prime, then \(S_P(N)=1\) for \(N\ge1\), forcing \(\zeta(2)=1/c_P\) to be rational.

Checked implication: if \(\pi^2\) is irrational, then for every natural bound there is a larger prime. Basel and the Euler-product argument are proved; irrationality of \(\pi^2\) remains an explicit hypothesis. Irrationality of \(\pi\) alone would not suffice. The dependency audit confirms that this proof uses the Euler sieve and does not invoke the existing Euclid infinitude theorem.

Lean sources: Basel, real-input compatibility, prime infinitude implication, and dependency audit.

Beyond Basel: Euler’s even values

The classical general formula is

\[\zeta(2n)=\frac{(2\pi)^{2n}|B_{2n}|}{2(2n)!},\qquad n\ge1.\]

Here \(B_{2n}\) is a Bernoulli number; see DLMF, equation 25.6.2. The general formula is not yet proved in the project. The case \(n=1\) is now the proved Basel theorem above.

The current boundary

This is a reference construction, with coarse bounds and no claim of numerical efficiency near one. Powers are constructed by the convergent binomial series. A separate proof equating that computation to \(\exp(-s\log n)\), parameter derivatives, general Euler products, and analytic continuation remain open. The differential equation above is a formal coefficient theorem, not an analytic derivative theorem.

The construction uses the existing rational interval foundation. It adds no completed-real type, Mathlib dependency, or new axiom. The published audit records inherited foundational dependencies, including existing native-decision certificates. Identifying Apéry’s rational approximants with \(\zeta(3)\) remains a separate task.
