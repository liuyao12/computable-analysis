Preface

A notebook on Observable

This project began as a programming exercise on Observable. JavaScript had just acquired BigInt, so I could work with exact fractions in a browser notebook. I wanted to see whether I could compute familiar real numbers to any requested precision using only rational arithmetic.

The notebook interface was simple. Give the computation a positive rational tolerance and get back two fractions that enclose the answer. The gap between them tells you how much uncertainty remains.

Notebook sketch

enclose(value, tolerance)

Two rational endpoints, with a gap smaller than the requested tolerance.

From a notebook to a calculus book

It struck me that a Riemann sum could work the same way. If I could bound the error, a finite calculation already gave me something I could use. I imagined this as an exercise at the end of a calculus course. Pick a number or function you know, turn it into a computation, and explain how to make the error as small as you like.

The fractions grew unwieldy, and I put the notebook aside. When I came back to it, I let each computation take a stage number. That left the choice of how to refine to the computation itself. The mathematics was in proving that the rational bounds keep closing in. Lean gave me a way to check those proofs without ever having to print the last fraction.

A first look at a real-number computation

Start with \([1,2]\) for \(\sqrt{2}\). Draw a secant through the corresponding points on \(y=x^2\) and a tangent at the right endpoint. Their intersections with \(y=2\) give the next lower and upper bounds, \([4/3,3/2]\).
