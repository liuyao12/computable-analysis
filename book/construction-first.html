General laws about particular constructions

Begin with objects whose constructions and mathematical properties have been justified. Then prove what follows from those properties. A useful general theorem need not wait for a theorem producing every possible input under the weakest possible conditions.

Given the constructed objects and the stated evidence about them, the conclusion follows.

Here, “constructed” means more than giving something a name. A number needs valid approximations; a function needs a domain; a derivative, integral, root, or solution needs a proof connecting its computation to that role. The conclusion must add something to the supplied evidence.

TaskWhat the proof establishesWhat remains separate
Construct an objectA particular computation has the required mathematical properties.A universal construction for every classical input.
Prove a general lawSupplied objects and their proved properties imply a new conclusion.The existence of every object to which the law could apply.
Compare constructionsIndependent computations give the same value or function.An identity already included among the hypotheses.

A conditional theorem is a completed theorem on its stated domain. For a concrete application, we must still construct the objects and prove the hypotheses. Writing down an interface containing the desired conclusion does not prove that conclusion.

A solution can be studied before a general solver exists

The checked linear-system growth theorem starts with a supplied solution, valid vector approximations, and a local estimate certifying its differential equation. If the matrix satisfies a column-sum bound \(\lVert tA(t)\rVert_1\le N\), it derives, for rational \(0<a\le b\le R\),

\[\lVert y(b)\rVert_1\le M\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lVert y(a)\rVert_1\le \frac{b^N M}{a^N}.\]

The growth bound is a conclusion, not part of the supplied solution data. This theorem does not require a universal ODE solver, and it does not claim the full complex Fuchs classification.

A representation can support another representation

The checked Cauchy-series theorem starts with a certified Cauchy-kernel representation and convergent boundary moments. It constructs an independent power-series computation and proves equality at rational complex points inside the certified disk. Deriving that Cauchy representation from complex differentiability is another theorem. The supplied representation is not already a Taylor expansion.

Methods belong in the formalization skill

Whole-chunk rectangles, alternating brackets, tail estimates, bisection, and finite differential models are methods to choose when formalizing an example. Their finite algorithms and proofs belong in Lean. A reusable record may organize the evidence, but it is not a compulsory definition of every integral, sum, or solution.

We retain exact theorem statements, genuine domain and branch restrictions, and agreement between representations. We retain proved existence theorems too. The change is in how we choose the next useful result: a law with explicit, usable hypotheses can stand on its own.
