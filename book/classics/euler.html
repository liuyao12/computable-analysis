Euler’s sine-product proof

Euler’s sine product determines every positive even zeta value. The Basel case follows from a finite-product remainder; the general theorem follows from a justified logarithmic derivative and coefficient comparison.

\[\boxed{\zeta(2m)=\frac{(-1)^{m+1}B_{2m}(2\pi)^{2m}}{2(2m)!}}\qquad(m\ge1).\]

This is a checked Lean proof for every positive integer \(m\), using Mathlib’s sine-product theorem and its locally uniform logarithmic derivative. Here \(B_{2m}\) denotes a Bernoulli number and \(\zeta(2m)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-2m}\). It is independent of the Bernoulli–Fourier evaluation and of our native finite-rearrangement proof. The proof runs in a separate Mathlib package; it does not yet supply an Euler-product proof in the project’s rational-interval foundation.

What has to be justified?

Knowing the zeros of sine does not by itself determine the function: multiplication by a nonvanishing factor preserves those zeros. Even after the product identity has been proved, an infinite product cannot simply be expanded like a polynomial without controlling its remainder.

\[\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{x^2}{k^2}\right).\]

We justify these two steps separately: use a proved sine-product identity, then extract its quadratic coefficient by an explicit inequality. No differentiation of an infinite product is needed for the Basel case.

The Basel case: start with a finite product

For a finite family with \(0\le a_k\le1\), set \(A=\sum_k a_k\). Induction proves

\[0\le\prod_k(1-a_k)-(1-A)\le\frac{A^2}{2}.\]

If \(R\) is the current remainder, adjoining a factor gives the exact identity \(R_{\mathrm{new}}=(1-a)R+aA\). Nonnegativity and the upper bound follow using only finite algebra and inequalities.

Now put

\[S_N=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac1{k^2},\qquad P_N(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{x^2}{k^2}\right).\]

The telescoping comparison \(1/k^2\le2/k-2/(k+1)\) gives \(0\le S_N\le2\). Consequently, for \(0\le x\le1\),

\[\boxed{0\le P_N(x)-(1-x^2S_N)\le2x^4.}\]

The bound is uniform in \(N\). This is the crucial replacement for discarding unspecified higher-order terms.

Supply the sine-product identity

Mathlib proves the product formula by a recurrence for weighted cosine integrals, followed by a concentration argument related to Wallis’ formula. This supplies the analytic identity without appealing to a previously evaluated reciprocal-square sum. General Weierstrass factorization is not required for this route.

Write \(S=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}1/k^2\); ordinary series convergence does not assume its value. Fix \(0<x\le1\) and pass to the limit in the finite estimate:

\[0\le\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}-(1-x^2S)\le2x^4.\]

Compare coefficients with an error bound

The independently proved sine estimate is

\[\left|\sin t-\left(t-\frac{t^3}{6}\right)\right|\le\frac{|t|^5}{100}\qquad(|t|\le1).\]

Combine this with the product estimate and divide by the positive quadratic factor. For \(0<x\le1\) and \(\pi x\le1\), Lean proves

\[\boxed{\left|S-\frac{\pi^2}{6}\right|\le\left(2+\frac{\pi^4}{100}\right)x^2.}\]

Take \(x=1/((\pi+1)(n+1))\) and let \(n\) grow. The right side tends to zero, so \(S=\pi^2/6\). Euler’s coefficient comparison is now a consequence of two controlled remainders.

Every positive even value

For the general case, differentiate the sine product logarithmically. Mathlib proves local uniform convergence away from the integers and justifies convergence of the logarithmic derivatives. This gives the cotangent partial-fraction expansion, without a Fourier or zeta-value theorem.

\[\pi\cot(\pi z)-\frac1z=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac1{z-k}+\frac1{z+k}\right).\]

Expand each paired fraction geometrically. To exchange the two sums, the new proof establishes the summable bound

\[\left|\frac{(1+(-1)^r)z^r}{k^{r+2}}\right|\le\frac{2|z|^r}{k^2}\qquad(k\ge1,\ |z|<1).\]

Define the convergent series

\[A_r=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac1{k^{r+2}},\qquad Q(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(1+(-1)^r)A_rz^r.\]

The checked sum interchange gives

\[\pi z\cot(\pi z)=1-z^2Q(z)\qquad(0<|z|<1).\]

Thus the coefficient of \(z^{2m}\) on the right is \(-2\zeta(2m)\). Both the analytic identity and the coefficient extraction are formalized.

Identify the Bernoulli coefficients

Set \(w=2\pi i z\). The exponential formula for cotangent gives

\[\pi z\cot(\pi z)=\frac{w}{e^w-1}+\frac w2.\]

The Bernoulli numbers are characterized algebraically by their formal generating function:

\[\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_j}{j!}w^j\right)(e^w-1)=w.\]

Our proof transports the analytic identity to an equality of formal power series using uniqueness of convergent power-series coefficients. It then uses the Bernoulli generating identity and cancellation in the formal power-series ring. Comparing the coefficient of \(z^{2m}\) proves the boxed formula for every \(m\ge1\).

The latter three identities are checked specializations of the general theorem, with the Bernoulli numbers evaluated by their finite recurrence. The earlier Basel proof remains available independently.

What is checked?

The general theorem is unconditional. Its audit follows actual elaborated proof dependencies and requires the sine product, justified logarithmic derivative, double-series summability, coefficient comparison, and Bernoulli generating identity. It rejects Fourier dependencies, previous zeta evaluations, the earlier Basel evaluation, unfinished proofs, and custom axioms. The permitted axioms are Mathlib’s usual propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound.
