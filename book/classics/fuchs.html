Fuchs’s theorem

A condition on the coefficients determines how solutions behave near a singular point.

Setup

Fix a complex point \(a\). Let \(p\) and \(q\) be meromorphic near \(a\), with no other singularities in a sufficiently small punctured disk, and consider

\[y''+p(z)y'+q(z)y=0.\]

A solution has moderate growth at \(a\) if, on each fixed branch and each closed angular subsector of finite opening, there are constants \(C>0\) and a nonnegative integer \(N\) such that \(|y(z)|\le C|z-a|^{-N}\) for all sufficiently small \(|z-a|>0\). The constants may depend on the solution, branch, and subsector.

Theorem — Fuchs’s criterion

Every local solution has moderate growth at \(a\) if and only if both coefficient functions

\[(z-a)p(z)\quad\text{and}\quad(z-a)^2q(z)\]

extend holomorphically across \(a\). Equivalently, the first-derivative coefficient has at most a simple pole and the zeroth-order coefficient has at most a double pole.

This includes ordinary points. When \(a\) is singular, the solution-growth condition says that it is a regular singular point. Thus the theorem connects a test on coefficients to actual solution behavior. Frobenius theory further constructs solutions from convergent power series, powers, and, when needed, logarithms. Monodromy describes the branching. Continue a basis of local solutions around a loop avoiding the coefficient singularities and compare it with the initial basis. The resulting invertible matrix is its monodromy matrix. Regular singularity permits nontrivial monodromy: the Bessel example returns its logarithmic solution with an added multiple of the regular solution. Growth and return of branches are distinct properties.

What our formalization proves

The forward growth theorem is checked. For polynomial coefficients in the scaled equation

\[z^2y''+zp(z)y'+q(z)y=0,\]

a certified solution along a fixed rational complex ray has a computed polynomial bound toward the origin. With the scaled jet \(Y=(y,zy')\), suitable computed \(N\) and an outer-endpoint bound \(M\) give

\[\lVert Y(a)\rVert_1\le\frac{b^N M}{a^N},\qquad 0<a\le b.\]

Here \(a,b\) are positive ray parameters. The definition of solution requires finite-difference derivative estimates; it does not assume this growth bound. The proof uses rational interval computations.

We also prove the nonresonant formal Frobenius recurrence, a convergence certificate for the larger rational indicial root, and all-degree Laguerre polynomial solutions.

Remaining scope: the full converse, general complex-sector theory, and a complete treatment of logarithmic branches are not yet formalized.

A checked continuation application

The solution \(y(z)=z^2\) of \(z^2y''-zy'=0\) now connects the continuation and growth developments. Its complex derivatives are proved at every valid represented input. Along a two-edge route, the computation changes from \(z^2\) to \((z+1)^2-2z-1\); the formulas agree on a neighborhood of the transition point. Equality of derivative germs transfers the second-order equation to the changed chart.

The existing Frobenius solver gives its exponent-two factor: constant coefficient one and all higher coefficients zero. On the positive real ray, exact comparison theorems identify the holomorphic scaled jet \((y,zy')\) with a certified input to the growth theorem. Its finite-difference remainder has norm \(3|h|^2\), so radius \(\varepsilon/3\) suffices. The computed growth bound is \(\lVert Y(a)\rVert_1\le6/a^3\) for \(0\lt a\le1\).

This polynomial solution is entire and can cross the singular point of the normalized equation. General Frobenius branches still need complex-series and branch constructions. Pointwise holomorphicity also does not automatically supply the uniform interval estimates required by the ray-growth interface. General sector continuation and Bessel monodromy remain open.

Further theorems in this direction

Schwarz’s classification determines the Gauss hypergeometric equations with finite monodromy, equivalently with an algebraic basis of solutions. It turns local differential-equation data into a classification of algebraic functions. Hypergeometric monodromy and the classification.

Sturm’s separation and comparison theorems describe the zeros of real second-order linear equations: independent solutions have interlacing zeros, and coefficient inequalities control oscillation. This is a more elementary direction with direct geometric content. MIT’s separation and comparison lecture.

Kovacic’s algorithm decides whether a second-order linear equation with rational-function coefficients has a nonzero Liouvillian solution, built using algebraic extensions, integrals, and exponentials of integrals. A negative result rules out that entire class of solutions. Kovacic’s original paper.

These are candidate developments, not claims of completed formalizations in this project.
