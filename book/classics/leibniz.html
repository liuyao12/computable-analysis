The Leibniz series

One geometric circle constant, three native proof routes, and a comparison with Mathlib.

Setup

Let \(\pi\) be the circle constant obtained from geometric exhaustion. For each nonnegative integer \(N\), form the rational partial sum

\[S_N=4\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}.\]

Theorem — Leibniz

The partial sums converge to the geometric circle constant. More precisely, for every nonnegative integer \(N\), their error is at most four times the first omitted denominator’s reciprocal:

\[|S_N-\pi|\le\frac{4}{2N+3}.\]

Consequently, the alternating sum of the reciprocals of the positive odd integers equals one quarter of the circle constant:

\[\frac{\pi}{4}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}.\]

Finite sums and a usable error bound

Put \(S_N=4\sum_{k=0}^N(-1)^k/(2k+1)\). Alternating upper and lower sums enclose the answer, with the checked estimate

\[|S_N-\pi|\le\frac4{2N+3}.\]

The formal statement compares rational enclosures at every independently chosen geometric stage. It separates the series computation, its validity, and its agreement with geometric \(\pi\).

Three native routes

Finite rectangle comparison

Finite rational identities and a mesh-error estimate connect the alternating sums to geometric exhaustion. The proof does not use the Taylor/FTC comparison theorem.

Computation, remainder, and geometric identification

Polynomial FTC

Integrate a finite geometric identity, using certified monomial primitives:

\[\frac1{1+x^2}=\sum_{k=0}^N(-1)^k x^{2k}+(-1)^{N+1}\frac{x^{2N+2}}{1+x^2}.\]

Integral order bounds the remainder by \(1/(2N+3)\) on \([0,1]\).

Comparison with Mathlib

Mathlib’s Real.tendsto_sum_pi_div_four starts with the arctangent power series inside its disk of convergence. Abel’s endpoint theorem extends the identity to the boundary point \(1\). The result states convergence of finite sums in Mathlib’s real numbers.

Our native routes use rational boxes and explicit finite error estimates. Mathlib reuses its general theory of real and complex power series. These are different formal routes to the displayed identity; the existing map describes their mathematical dependencies without asserting a checked representation bridge between the two real-number foundations.
