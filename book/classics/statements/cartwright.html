Setup

Let \(\pi\) be the circle constant obtained from geometric exhaustion. A number is rational if it equals a quotient of two integers with nonzero denominator.

Theorem — Irrationality of the squared circle constant

The square of the geometric circle constant is irrational: for every integer \(a\) and every nonzero integer \(b\),

\[\pi^2\ne\frac{a}{b}.\]

In the computable foundation, this means that the computation of \(\pi^2\) is not equivalent to any constant rational computation.
