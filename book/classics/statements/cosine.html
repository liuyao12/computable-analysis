Setup

Let \(\pi\) be the geometric circle constant, and let \(C(x)\) be the cosine coordinate on the circle, normalized so that \(C(x)=\cos(\pi x)\). On the rational interval \([0,1/2]\), construct the integral of \(C(x)^2\) from refining rectangle sums.

Theorem — The cosine-square integral

The integral of the squared cosine coordinate over this quarter turn is exactly one quarter:

\[\int_0^{1/2}C(x)^2\,dx=\int_0^{1/2}\cos^2(\pi x)\,dx=\frac14.\]

Equivalently, its average value on this interval is one half.
