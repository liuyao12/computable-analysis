Setup

Let \(\pi\) be the geometric circle constant and put \(c(t)=\cos(\pi t/2)\) on \([0,1]\). For nonnegative integers \(m,n\), define

\[W_n=\int_0^1c(t)^n\,dt,\qquad B_{m,n}=\int_0^1t^m(1-t)^n\,dt.\]

Both integrals are constructed by refining rational sample sums. Let \(R_0=1\) and, for positive integers \(n\), let

\[R_n=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(2k)^2}{(2k-1)(2k+1)}.\]

Theorems — Wallis and beta integrals

For every nonnegative integer \(n\), the even and odd cosine-power integrals have these exact factorial values:

\[W_{2n}=\frac{(2n)!}{4^n(n!)^2},\qquad W_{2n+1}=\frac{2\cdot4^n(n!)^2}{\pi(2n+1)!}.\]

The finite Wallis products enclose the circle constant, for every nonnegative integer \(n\):

\[2R_n\le\pi\le2R_n\frac{2n+2}{2n+1}.\]

In particular, \(R_n\) converges to \(\pi/2\). For every pair of nonnegative integers \(m,n\), including zero exponents, the beta integral is

\[B_{m,n}=\frac{m!n!}{(m+n+1)!}.\]

Consequently, multiplying its integrand by \((m+n+1)!/(m!n!)\) gives a polynomial density with integral one.
