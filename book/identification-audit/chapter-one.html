The computation’s validity and its mathematical identity are separate facts. For the square-root function, the identifying law is

\[S(q)\ge0,\qquad S(q)\cdot S(q)\simeq q\qquad(q\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}).\]

This makes \(S\) the nonnegative inverse of squaring and covers every nonnegative integer, including \(2\), in one theorem. Once the law is proved, we can use the square root without inspecting its algorithm. The main library proves this identity for its rational bisection computation; other constructions can establish their own identity or prove agreement with it.
