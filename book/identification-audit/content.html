A computation needs a mathematical identity

First prove that the intervals represent a value. Then prove the laws that tell us what that value is.

From a valid algorithm to a mathematical object

Ordered, nested rational intervals whose widths shrink to zero give a valid represented value. The next question is mathematical: why does this computation deserve the name we gave it? Its identifying laws should be usable without inspecting the program, even when their proofs work through all the program’s rational inequalities.

This is the project’s strategy for numbers, functions, derivatives, integrals and solutions. Domain, sign, normalization, branch and initial conditions remain part of the claim. Comparison and uniqueness need their own justified arguments where they are claimed.

One square-root law covers all the examples

The square-root function provides the model. On nonnegative rational inputs, prove

\[S(q)\ge0,\qquad S(q)\cdot S(q)\simeq q\qquad(q\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}).\]

This identifies the nonnegative inverse of squaring. Every nonnegative integer is included; the identity at \(2\) is a specialization. A new algorithm can earn the same interpretation through its own proof or a justified comparison with this one.

The main library already has the general rational square-root identity and a separate validity theorem. Its particular inverse-search adapter is stated on \([0,1]\). The foundation proof bundle additionally identifies the secant–tangent computation at \(2\). These are different scopes of a shared strategy.
