2.12 Volume in every dimension, by rational polytopes

A sphere is the boundary; the quantity computed here is the volume of its solid ball. Write \(B_n(R)=\{x:\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\le R^2\}\), and write \(v_n\) for the volume of \(B_n(1)\). The construction below uses rational boundary points, exact rational polytope volumes, and an explicit refinement bound. The familiar formulas will follow from this independently defined volume.

The finite volume axioms come first

We keep the chapter’s orientation convention: reversing orientation negates volume. In each positive dimension, volume is finitely additive under oriented dissections, invariant under translations and linear maps of determinant one, and normalized on the standard oriented cube by

\[ \operatorname{Vol}_n([0,1]^n)=1. \]

Dissection additivity applies when intersections have dimension below the ambient dimension. It follows that lower-dimensional pieces have zero volume: applying this additivity to a lower-dimensional piece and itself gives \(V=V+V\), hence \(V=0\). Positively oriented regions have nonnegative volume; finite additivity therefore gives monotonicity for containment with matching positive orientations. All balls and enclosing polytopes below use that orientation.

Rational boxes. Partition the unit cube into congruent boxes using a common positive integer denominator. Translation invariance and finite additivity give every small box its rational product of side lengths. Cutting and assembling these boxes proves

\[ \operatorname{Vol}_n\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^n[a_i,b_i]\right) =\prod_{i=1}^n(b_i-a_i),\qquad a_i,b_i\in\mathbb Q,\quad a_i\le b_i. \]

Rational stretching is a consequence. For \(q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), let \(D_q=\operatorname{diag}(q,1,\ldots,1)\). Then

\[ \operatorname{Vol}_n(D_qP)=q\operatorname{Vol}_n(P). \]

Here is the justification without assuming a determinant transformation law. Enclose a rational polytope \(P\subset[-C,C]^n\) by the rational grid boxes of side \(1/N\) that meet it, and inscribe the boxes wholly inside it. The remaining boxes meet its finitely many facet hyperplanes. For a hyperplane \(a\cdot x=b\), choose a coordinate with maximal \(|a_i|\). Over each grid column in the other coordinates the hyperplane meets at most \(n+2\) boxes. If there are \(F\) facets and \(C\ge1\) is rational, the total volume gap is at most

\[ \frac{F(n+2)(2C+2)^{n-1}}{N}. \]

The stretched boxes have exactly \(q\) times the original box volumes, with the same finite incidence relations. Their gap is at most \(q\) times this bound. Both sides of the claimed identity therefore agree to every prescribed rational error. All box classification is finite rational linear feasibility; there is no existence claim based on a completed scalar field.

The simplex formula is proved, not stipulated

Let \(\Delta_n=\{x_i\ge0:\sum_i x_i\le1\}\). The unit cube is dissected into the \(n!\) ordered-coordinate simplices

\[ 0\le x_{\sigma(1)}\le\cdots\le x_{\sigma(n)}\le1, \qquad \sigma\in S_n. \]

Each has vertices given by the successive terminal sums of coordinate basis vectors. Order these vertices to give the orientation inherited from the cube. The resulting edge matrix has determinant one: when the first ordering has determinant negative one, exchange two vertices. Thus every oriented piece is a determinant-one affine image of the standard oriented simplex. The stated invariances give equal volumes to all pieces, so additivity and cube normalization imply

\[ \operatorname{Vol}_n(\Delta_n)=\frac1{n!}. \]

For rational vertices \(p_0,\ldots,p_n\), put \(A=[p_1-p_0\ \cdots\ p_n-p_0]\). If \(\det A>0\), put \(q=\det A\). Then \(A=S D_q\) with \(\det S=1\), so rational stretching and the given invariances give volume \(\det A/n!\). If \(\det A<0\), exchange two vertices: both the volume and determinant change sign, reducing to the positive case. Consequently, with orientation given by the displayed vertex order,

\[ \boxed{\operatorname{Vol}_n\bigl(\operatorname{conv}(p_0,\ldots,p_n)\bigr) =\frac{\det(p_1-p_0,\ldots,p_n-p_0)}{n!}.} \]

If the determinant vanishes, the simplex is lower-dimensional and has volume zero. Thus the same formula covers degenerate simplices. A positively oriented rational convex polytope is triangulated by triangulating its faces and coning them to the rational mean of its vertices. Recursion in dimension gives finitely many consistently oriented rational simplices. The axioms prove that the sum of their determinant volumes is independent of the triangulation. This establishes exact rational polytope volume before we use it to bound a ball.

What is the polytope here?

For this construction, a finite rational vertex set and its convex hull suffice for the inner body. The outer body is a bounded intersection of finitely many rational halfspaces. These describe the geometric bodies; a triangulation is a way to compute their volumes, rather than the definition of the bodies. Both carry the standard ambient orientation. Finite differences and unions used in the proof are polytopal regions, dissected into consistently oriented convex pieces.

Mathlib’s generic convexHull can describe the hull on rational coordinates. We do not need a new general polytope type for this example. Rational coordinates provide the exact finite geometry and its dense input domain; interpreting the same inequalities on valid represented coordinates is a separate bridge. Mathlib’s set description supplies no volume theorem for our foundation. In the checked rational layer, points have type Fin n → ℚ, the inner body uses convexHull ℚ, and a finite halfspace presentation uses rational normals and rational offsets. The sphere’s outer body is the special case with offset one. Including each positive and negative coordinate-axis point bounds every outer coordinate between \(-1\) and \(1\).

Rational points on the boundary

For \(n\ge2\), take \(t\in\mathbb Q^{n-1}\), put \(s=\sum_i t_i^2\), and use the two charts

\[ \sigma_\pm(t)=\frac{(2t_1,\ldots,2t_{n-1},\ \pm(1-s))}{1+s}. \]

Expansion gives \(\|\sigma_\pm(t)\|^2=1\) exactly. The two charts cover the northern and southern hemispheres with parameters in the unit parameter ball, hence in \([-1,1]^{n-1}\). On either chart the finite identity

\[ \|\sigma_\pm(t)-\sigma_\pm(u)\|^2 =\frac{4\|t-u\|^2}{(1+\|t\|^2)(1+\|u\|^2)} \le4\|t-u\|^2 \]

gives an explicit sampling schedule. Take all parameters \(t_i=j_i/M\), \(-M\le j_i\le M\), in both charts. Nearest-coordinate rounding shows that every unit direction is within squared distance \((n-1)/M^2\) of a sampled point. Use \(M=2^k\) so the finite sample sets \(S_M\) are nested. The coordinate axis points are already present.

Exact inner and outer polytopes

\[ P_M=\operatorname{conv}(S_M),\qquad Q_M=\bigcap_{p\in S_M}\{x:p\cdot x\le1\}. \]

Every sampled point lies on the unit boundary. Every outer plane is its exact tangent plane. Consequently \(P_M\subset B_n(1)\subset Q_M\); axis tangents make \(Q_M\) bounded. Vertices of \(Q_M\) are solutions of rational linear systems, so both polytopes have exactly computable rational volumes

\[ L_M=\operatorname{Vol}_n(P_M),\qquad U_M=\operatorname{Vol}_n(Q_M). \]

There is also a rational stopping rule. Put \(\eta=(n-1)/(2M^2)\) and \(a=1-\eta>0\). For a unit direction \(u\), the nearby point \(p\) has \(u\cdot p=1-\|u-p\|^2/2\ge a\). Applying this to each outward facet normal of \(P_M\) proves \(aB_n(1)\subset P_M\). Applying it to the direction of a nonzero point of \(Q_M\) proves \(Q_M\subset a^{-1}B_n(1)\). Hence

\[ Q_M\subset a^{-2}P_M,\qquad 0\le U_M-L_M\le2^n(a^{-2n}-1). \]

This uses the polytope scaling law and \(P_M\subset[-1,1]^n\), not a prior ball-volume formula. For \(\eta\le1/(4n)\), the finite Bernoulli inequality \((1-\eta)^{2n}\ge1-2n\eta\) sharpens the stopping rule to

\[ \boxed{U_M-L_M\le\frac{2^{n+1}n(n-1)}{M^2}.} \]

Refinement increases \(L_M\) and decreases \(U_M\). The intervals therefore define a valid computable volume \(v_n\) with an explicit rational precision schedule. Any other correctly enclosing polytopes with shrinking gaps give the same value: every inner polytope of either construction is contained in every outer polytope of the other. This proves agreement of the computations without assuming a ball volume in advance. In dimension one the interval already has volume \(v_1=2\); the zero-dimensional convention is \(v_0=1\).

The general-dimensional formula

The geometric computation just constructed satisfies

\[ \boxed{v_{n+2}=\frac{2\pi}{n+2}\,v_n,\qquad v_0=1,\quad v_1=2.} \]

Here \(\pi=v_2\) is the disk area already established in this chapter. Induction gives, for every nonnegative integer \(k\) and nonnegative radius \(R\),

\[ \boxed{V_{2k}(R)=\frac{\pi^k}{k!}R^{2k},\qquad V_{2k+1}(R)=\frac{2^{k+1}\pi^k}{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2k+1)}R^{2k+1}.} \]

The odd denominator is one when \(k=0\). In particular, \(V_2(R)=\pi R^2\), \(V_3(R)=4\pi R^3/3\), \(V_4(R)=\pi^2R^4/2\), and \(V_5(R)=8\pi^2R^5/15\). Rational radii scale every vertex and determinant exactly. An arbitrary valid represented radius is handled by its nonnegative rational lower and upper bounds; refine those bounds and the unit-ball polytopes together. The finite power identity below controls the scaling error and proves representation independence.

Proof of the recurrence by finite dissections

Fix \(n\ge1\) and write coordinates in dimension \(n+2\) as \((x,y)\), with \(x\) in dimension \(n\) and \(y\) in dimension two. Set \(t_j=j/N\) and \(h=1/N\). Over the radial shell \(t_j\le\|x\|\le t_{j+1}\), the permitted squared radius of the disk in the last two coordinates lies between \(1-t_{j+1}^2\) and \(1-t_j^2\). Thus finite shell–disk products give

\[ v_n\pi A_N\le v_{n+2}\le v_n\pi B_N, \]
\[ A_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-t_{j+1}^2)(t_{j+1}^n-t_j^n),\qquad B_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-t_j^2)(t_{j+1}^n-t_j^n). \]

These inequalities are justified by rational polytopal dissections, rather than a general principle about cross-sections. Approximate each nested ball by its inner and outer polytopes, form shell differences by cutting their finitely many rational faces, and triangulate. For the disk radii, use rational lower and upper square-root bounds, then rational disk polygons. Products are rational polytopal regions, whose volumes multiply: prove this first for grid boxes, then squeeze each factor by the boundary-box estimate above. For each fixed finite partition, these constructions make the additional error smaller than any prescribed rational tolerance. Disjoint shell products add, and their inner and outer containment gives the displayed bounds. Thus a curved shell or disk is never assigned an uncomputed exact rational volume.

Finite summation by parts, with zero endpoint terms, gives

\[ A_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}t_j^n(t_{j+1}^2-t_j^2) =2h\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}t_j^{n+1}+h^2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}t_j^n. \]

For \(0\le a\le b\), factor the difference of powers:

\[ b^m-a^m=(b-a)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}b^{m-1-i}a^i. \]

Every term lies between \(a^{m-1}\) and \(b^{m-1}\). With \(m=n+2\), summing over the partition telescopes to one. The difference between the upper and lower power sums is exactly \(h\), so

\[ \frac1{n+2}-h\le h\sum_j t_j^{n+1}\le\frac1{n+2}. \]

The remaining term of \(A_N\) is between zero and \(h\). Also

\[ 0\le B_N-A_N =\sum_j(t_{j+1}^2-t_j^2)(t_{j+1}^n-t_j^n)\le2h. \]

Consequently both bounds differ from \(2/(n+2)\) by at most \(5/N\). Since \(v_n\pi\le2^{n+2}\), the geometric volume differs from \(2\pi v_n/(n+2)\) by at most \(5\cdot2^{n+2}/N\), in addition to the freely chosen rational polytope approximation error. Refinement to every rational tolerance proves the exact recurrence. For \(n=0\), the same recurrence is simply \(v_2=\pi\), already established. Repeated substitution and finite induction give the two closed formulas.

Only rational determinants, finite dissections, square-root brackets and power identities have been used. No notion of an integral occurs in the argument. In the later Gamma-function chapter we will give a second proof and show that it agrees with this geometric computation.

Formalization: checked statements and remaining geometry

The general-dimensional rational chart identity, finite power and partition estimates, shell-gap identity, and summation-by-parts identity are checked in Lean using only native rational arithmetic. A separate pinned Mathlib layer checks convexity, the inner/outer rational-ball containments, refinement, and boundedness from axis samples, all on rational coordinates. It also checks the determinant coefficient’s translation, linear-transformation, and determinant-one laws. The coefficient is an algebraic expression; these identities do not derive it from the volume axioms.

The Mathlib layer is pinned to revision 51e6992efd06126df61a496bebf8f49482a4e129. Its actual transitive theorem-declaration dependencies contain no Mathlib real or complex scalars, measure, or integration. Broad module imports do include those modules, so this layer remains an isolated, reproducible package rather than joining the native foundation’s import closure.

The simplex-axiom derivation, triangulation independence, chart covering and quantitative containment estimates, product-volume law, and agreement with a valid represented ball volume are proved mathematically above but are not yet fully checked in Lean. The older FiniteNBallVolume.lean proves the algebra of a formula model; it does not by itself identify that model with this geometric volume. The exact 3D certificates are checked by the rational Python program, separately from Lean.
