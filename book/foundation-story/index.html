From numbers to functions

Begin by computing \(\sqrt2\) and \(\pi\). Recognize them as evaluations of rational-input functions, then build the tools for comparing different computations.

Convexity and concavity guide the proposal of an algorithm. Its formal validity is a separate proof by inequalities: target bounds, nesting, and shrinking. Irrational evaluations are derived add-ons.

The emphasis is on constructions and exact mathematical identities. Quantitative bounds justify them and remain available for numerical corollaries. When a numerical result is requested, specialize a checked computation, choose a sufficient stage from its error bound, and prove the resulting rational inequalities.

How to explore: choose a construction below. Follow its prerequisites, expand a theorem, and inspect the direct constants used in its checked proof or statement. The construction arrows explain the mathematical story; declaration dependencies come from Lean.
