Use convexity or concavity as an informal guide for proposing an interval update before plain bisection. Do not require a formal convexity predicate, a general tangent definition, or an inverse function theorem to execute this strategy. The separate formalization job proves the proposed update's validity directly from the particular rational formulas and finite evaluator bounds, using inequality proofs: target containment, nesting, and an explicit shrinking schedule. Establish only the supporting-line or secant inequalities actually needed. Estimate the rate after proving these invariants.

For square root, the square function is convex on the positive branch. Given \(0<a\le b\) and \(a^2\le q\le b^2\), the right-end supporting line \(2bt-b^2\) and the endpoint secant \((a+b)t-ab\) give \[ b' = \frac{b^2+q}{2b},\qquad a' = \frac{q+ab}{a+b}. \] Prove \(a\le a'\le b'\le b\) and \((a')^2\le q\le(b')^2\) by rational algebra. Moreover, \[ b'-a'=\frac{(b-a)(b^2-q)}{2b(a+b)} \le\frac{(b-a)^2}{2b}\le\frac{b-a}{2}. \] Starting at \([1,2]\) computes \(\sqrt2\). The last bound proves shrinking; a positive lower bound for \(b\) makes the preceding bound a quadratic width estimate. Compare the resulting valid name with the existing bisection computation by their squared-value bounds, rather than assuming agreement.

For arctangent on a nonnegative branch, use its concavity: the endpoint secant lies below the function and a justified supporting line lies above it. Their target intersections therefore bound the inverse in the opposite order from the convex example. Endpoint values are represented numbers, so enclose the proposed intersections with rational arithmetic and retain an overlap-safe shrinking step. Never rely on deciding exact equality of an interval-computed midpoint and target.

For circle trigonometry specify \(\arctan(u)=\theta/2\), then construct \[ \sin\theta=\frac{2u}{1+u^2},\qquad \cos\theta=\frac{1-u^2}{1+u^2}. \] Keep rational angle coordinates primary (for example a rational multiple of the constructed quarter-turn); derive other target evaluations with the proved continuity evidence. The equation \(\arctan(x)=x\) is a different problem, with only the zero solution, and does not construct general circle coordinates. Handle signs and other quadrants through explicit branch transformations.

Rational evaluations may support exact sign comparisons. Represented evaluations require interval separation or a justified overlapping-bracket alternative; do not wait for an arbitrary exact-zero decision. An initial sign bracket alone does not justify every subdivision decision or coherent shrinking preimages.

InverseBisectionSearch already demands validity, domain containment, and output overlap. Construct those fields from the actual function; merely repackaging assumed search evidence is not a new existence proof. A finite bracket with tolerance is an intermediate result until its coherent represented value and exact target identity are proved.

Complex FTA supplies a complex root, not a real root in the requested interval. Prove realness and interval membership independently when those are part of the task. Branch selection can depend on additional separation data.
