Analysis by construction.

A foundation for calculus in which the numbers, functions and evolutions we use are computations—with proofs that they work.

Much of science and engineering asks for a value, a trajectory, or a controlled approximation. This book starts with rational arithmetic and develops the mathematical constructions that supply them. An infinite process is accompanied by finite enclosures and a proof that they become as accurate as required.

The purpose is not to reproduce every theorem about unrestricted real-valued functions. It is to develop enough elementary and special-function calculus for substantial scientific work without obtaining numerical objects from noncomputable declarations. Domain conditions, separation from zero, convergence estimates and branch choices are part of the mathematics, stated where they matter.

Start with numbers, then geometry

The first two chapters retain the original exposition. A number is a terminating rational-enclosure computation, and equality is a relation between computations. Finite geometry then builds the circle, the sphere and the cylinder before invoking trigonometry or a general integral.

Read the mathematics; inspect a proof when it helps

The text presents definitions, theorem statements and the ideas of their proofs. A link in a theorem’s margin opens a proof map. Each node collects a useful group of mathematical declarations rather than a single implementation detail. Follow the argument at that level, then open the exact Lean statements when precision is needed.

The first fully paired example is the cosine primitive. Its direct inequality proof, native fundamental-theorem proof, and Mathlib comparison prove one proposition about the same two computations. The Mathlib development lives in a separate package. It identifies our constructions with familiar real analysis without becoming a prerequisite of the native route.

A developing foundation, not a finished claim of coverage

The later chapters chart a route through series, local models, transforms, complex paths and differential equations. Their mathematical exposition is retained; a theorem’s proof map distinguishes a checked comparison from an editorial outline. A manuscript theorem is not automatically advertised as a completely linked Lean result.

The computational boundary is also explicit. The currently selected native constructions are audited separately from older compatibility code. The historical source tree still contains noncomputable helpers that require replacement or isolation; the project does not hide them behind a “computable” label. See the programme and current boundary.

The aim is a mathematical book that can be read on its own—and a formal development whose constructions and proof dependencies can be inspected, compared and reused.
