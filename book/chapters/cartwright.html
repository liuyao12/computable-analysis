An arithmetic proof with calculus in the middle

Construct an integer sequence, use cosine moments to bound it, then finish with finite arithmetic.

Development status. The denominator-clearing recurrence and the final integer obstruction below are checked in Lean. The weighted integral construction and its alternative evaluation proofs are the next tasks. This chapter is a proof plan with a verified arithmetic component, not a completed formal proof of irrationality.

Arithmetic before the integral

Define \(P_0(z)=P_1(z)=1\) and \(P_{n+2}(z)=(2n+3)P_{n+1}(z)-zP_n(z)\). At a rational input \(z=a/b\), denominators can be cleared without a general polynomial-degree argument. The integer recurrence

\[K_0=1,\quad K_1=b,\quad K_{n+2}=(2n+3)bK_{n+1}-abK_n\]

satisfies \(K_n=b^nP_n(a/b)\). Both the recurrence and this exact rational identity are now checked. They mention neither trigonometry nor computable real-number programs.

What the calculus must supply

Write \(\lambda=\pi/2\) for the existing geometric pi, and independently construct

\[J_n=\int_0^1(1-t^2)^n C(t/2)\,dt.\]

The integrands are nonnegative and decreasing. The weighted samples need one explicitly successful joint subdivision/evaluation schedule, not a new predicate classifying all integrable functions. Polynomial weights between zero and one do not enlarge the cosine sample widths.

The intended evaluation is

\[\lambda^{2n+1}J_n\simeq 2^n n! P_n(\lambda^2),\qquad 0<J_n\le1.\]

Integration by parts twice yields the moment recurrence. A direct finite-sum proof and a Mathlib proof with value and quadrature bridges should establish the same computational statement. Those alternatives are planned, not yet measured. The current Mathlib pi-irrationality source already uses this moment family and integration-by-parts recurrence; its supporting private lemmas require an appropriate public comparison interface.

Under the hypothetical equality \(\lambda^2=a/b\), the precise output needed by the arithmetic is

\[0<K_n,\qquad K_n\,2^n n!\le2a^n.\]

The factor two uses \(0<\lambda\le2\). The proof of these inequalities is the boundary between the analytic construction and the arithmetic consumer.

Finite arithmetic after the integral

Choose the explicit index \(N=2a^2\), where \(a\) is a positive integer. The last \(a^2\) factors of \(N!\) give \(N!\ge(a^2)^{a^2}=a^N\). Also \(2^N>2\). Consequently

\[2a^N<2^N N!.\]

A positive integer \(K_N\) is at least one, so it cannot satisfy \(K_N2^NN!\le2a^N\). This finite contradiction is checked in Lean as a theorem about natural numbers and integers only. It makes no appeal to a limit, factorial asymptotics, or a completed real line.
