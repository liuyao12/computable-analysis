Arithmetic with calculus in the middle

A family of independently computed cosine integrals turns an integer recurrence into a proof that geometric π squared is irrational.

Begin with a finite recurrence

Define the integer polynomials by \(P_0(z)=P_1(z)=1\) and

\[P_{n+2}(z)=(2n+3)P_{n+1}(z)-zP_n(z).\]

For integers \(a,b\), denominator clearing is itself an integer algorithm:

\[K_0=1,\quad K_1=b,\quad K_{n+2}=(2n+3)bK_{n+1}-abK_n.\]

A finite induction proves \(K_n=b^nP_n(a/b)\), when \(b\ne0\). These constructions and that identity use only integer and rational arithmetic. They do not ask for the value of an integral.

Construct the moments before evaluating them

Keep our geometric normalization \(\pi=4A(1)\), and put \(\lambda=\pi/2\). On \([0,1]\), the cosine is \(c(t)=C(t/2)\). For every natural number \(n\), construct

\[J_n=\int_0^1(1-t^2)^n c(t)\,dt.\]

The integrand is nonnegative and decreasing. Its rational weight multiplies the rational-coordinate samples of the original inverse-arctangent cosine. It introduces no new trigonometric function and no nonrational integration endpoint.

The chosen stage \(k\) uses a dyadic mesh of depth \(k\) and evaluation stage \(k\). This choice is justified, not inferred from pointwise convergence. The proof separates a mesh error of at most \(2^{-k}\) from the accumulated evaluation uncertainty. Widen the sampled mean by \(113\,2^{-k}\), intersect its retained predecessors, and intersect with \([0,1]\). The resulting moment program is valid, with

\[\operatorname{width}((J_n)_k)\le226\,2^{-k}.\]

This bound holds for every \(n\). The implementation is a terminating construction with a proved schedule, not a claim of an optimized numerical algorithm. Neither the moment recurrence nor the proposed evaluation is used to prove its convergence.

Monotone sample bounds give, independently,

\[0<\frac1{16}\left(\frac{15}{16}\right)^n\le J_n\le1.\]

The calculus in the middle

TheoremWeighted cosine evaluation

For every \(n\in\mathbb N\), the independently constructed moment and polynomial computations satisfy

\[\boxed{\lambda^{2n+1}J_n\simeq2^n n!\,P_n(\lambda^2).}\]

The common intermediate laws are \(\lambda J_0=1\), \(\lambda^2J_1=2J_0\), and

\[\lambda^2J_{n+2}=2(n+2)(2n+3)J_{n+1}-4(n+2)(n+1)J_n.\]

After these analytic laws are established, scaling and the finite polynomial recurrence prove the evaluation by induction. The three formal derivations use the same moment program and have identical complete theorem types.

Finite summation by parts

For rational samples, expand the exact identity \(\Delta(fg)=f\,\Delta g+g\,\Delta f+\Delta f\,\Delta g\). Sum over a finite dyadic partition. Control the two increment-replacement errors and the quadratic cross-increment term, then refine. This route uses the local sine/cosine estimates but does not invoke the general FTC or the composite-primitive certificate.

A reusable quantitative FTC

The native FTC takes explicit local derivative bounds, not a completed real-line existence theorem. Polynomial products and the sine/cosine increment estimates certify the derivative of

\[F_n(t)=\lambda(1-t^2)^{n+2}s(t)-2(n+2)t(1-t^2)^{n+1}c(t).\]

Its derivative is the linear combination of weighted cosines in the recurrence. Applying the general finite-sample FTC and the endpoint bounds proves the law. The intermediate product need not itself be concave: this step uses the explicitly proved product/remainder certificates, not an unsupported application of the earlier concave-primitive theorem.

Mathlib and the quadrature bridge

The optional comparison first identifies these very sampled moments with Mathlib interval integrals. Integral order and finite additivity establish that bridge without an antiderivative. Mathlib's FTC and two integrations by parts then prove the moment laws. The final recurrence induction and arithmetic consumer are the same native declarations used by the other routes. No existing theorem of irrationality or transcendence of π is invoked.

Return to finite arithmetic

Suppose \(\lambda^2=a/b\), with positive integers \(a,b\). The evaluation and the independent bounds imply

\[0<K_n,\qquad K_n\,2^n n!\le2a^n.\]

That is all the arithmetic consumer needs. Choose the explicit index \(N=2a^2\). A finite factorial estimate gives \(2a^N<2^N N!\), whereas the positive integer \(K_N\) is at least one. The inequalities contradict each other.

TheoremIrrationality of geometric π squared

The square of the original geometric π computation is not equivalent to any constant rational program:

\[\boxed{\forall r\in\mathbb Q,\quad \pi^2\not\simeq r.}\]

Every displayed proof is closed: no unproved moment law, successful-schedule provider, or analytic estimate remains as a hypothesis.

The integer-obstruction lemma has no integral or computable-number dependency. The final instantiated irrationality proof still depends on the calculus that supplied its bounds. The map distinguishes those two statements rather than coloring the whole conclusion as calculus-free.
