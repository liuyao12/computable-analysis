Fuchs: coefficient bounds imply solution growth

z²y″ + zp(z)y′ + q(z)y = 0
Y = (y, zy′),   z = td

For complex rational polynomials p and q and a fixed nonzero rational complex direction d, the scaled jet satisfies a first-order system with matrix t⁻¹[[0, 1], [−q(td), 1−p(td)]]. Its column sums have a computed bound N/t, where N is a natural number obtained from the polynomial coefficients and an outer radius R.

Every solution of this system, certified by local finite differences on rational intervals away from zero, has polynomial growth toward the singularity:

‖Y(a)‖₁ ≤ bᴺ M / aᴺ,   0 < a ≤ b ≤ R.

The constant M comes from a single computed box at the outer endpoint b. The solution definition contains no growth bound. Its values may be non-rational: they are the same valid rational interval algorithms used in Chapter 1.

Where the analytic content lives

On each closed rational interval [a,b] away from zero, a positive rational step radius and an output-precision schedule certify

‖w − v − hA(x)v‖₁ ≤ ε|h|.

Here v and w are any rational samples in sufficiently refined boxes at x and x+h. The precision may depend on h. This gives the local differential equation independently of the desired growth conclusion.

The proof subdivides [a,b] into finitely many inward steps. The polynomial inequality sᴺ(t+N(t−s)) ≤ tᴺ⁺¹ absorbs the coefficient bound. Summing the remaining rational errors and making their budget arbitrarily small yields the theorem. No completed real line, complex-analytic library, exponential, logarithm, or integral is needed.

Lean checks a genuine pole example: y=1/t solves t²y″+2ty′=0, and the general theorem gives ‖(y,ty′)‖₁≤4/a⁴ on 0<a≤1. This bound is deliberately conservative. A second example uses a non-singleton series evaluator as a constant solution of Y′=0.

This is the forward growth implication along rational rays for polynomial regular coefficients. The converse Fuchs criterion, sector-uniform bounds, arbitrary holomorphic coefficients, and a general bridge from holomorphic second-order solutions to the certified scaled-jet system remain open. It is independent of the Frobenius recurrence below.

Euler–Fuchs: a coefficient obstruction

x y′ = r y   ⟹   (k − r)ck = 0

For a finite rational polynomial y = Σ ckxk, the coefficient equations hold exactly when every nonzero coefficient has degree k = r. We prove that this certificate implies the evaluated differential identity using the existing polynomial derivative. A nonzero polynomial solution therefore forces r to be a nonnegative integer.

Frobenius: a rational coefficient construction

x²y″ + xp(x)y′ + q(x)y = 0
I(s) = s(s − 1) + p₀s + q₀

For rational polynomial p and q, coefficient extraction gives a finite recurrence for the formal expansion y = xʳΣcₙxⁿ. A nonzero leading coefficient forces I(r) = 0. When I(r + n) ≠ 0 for every positive integer n, our rational algorithm constructs the unique formal solution with the prescribed leading coefficient.

Every finite prefix has zero residual coefficients below its cutoff. At a resonant index the remaining lower terms must vanish: for example, x²y″ + xy = 0 at exponent zero cannot have a nonzero leading term.

This formal coefficient theorem is separate from the convergence theorem below. A certified branch of xʳ is still open. The operator residual is defined independently of the recurrence, and its correctness theorem requires the nonzero denominators.

From coefficients to certified intervals

Suppose I(r) = 0 and the root gap A = 2r − 1 + p₀ is nonnegative. This covers the larger rational indicial root, including a repeated root. Then I(r + n) = n(n + A) ≥ n² for every positive integer n.

R = 1 + (|r| + 1)Σ|pⱼ| + Σ|qⱼ|
|cₙ| ≤ |c₀|Rⁿ

The checked recurrence implies this bound for every coefficient. For rational x with R|x| ≤ ½, write Sₙ(x) = Σk<ncₖxᵏ. The actual evaluator returns

[Sₙ(x) − 2|c₀|2⁻ⁿ, Sₙ(x) + 2|c₀|2⁻ⁿ].

These intervals are ordered and nested, contain every later finite partial sum, and have width 4|c₀|2⁻ⁿ. A rational precision request gives an explicit computation stage. This supplies a valid represented value for the Frobenius factor using the computable-analysis foundation.

For x²y″ + xy′ − x²y = 0, the factor begins 1 + x²/4 + x⁴/64 + ⋯. The theorem certifies all rational |x| ≤ 1/6. At x = 1/8, twelve terms give interval width 1/1024. A second regression uses alternating coefficients, so the tail proof handles signed series as well.

This theorem certifies the value of Σcₙxⁿ. Analytic derivative certificates, a branch of xʳ, and convergence for the smaller nonresonant root are still open. It does not classify algebraic solutions.

A complete polynomial family

xy″ + (b − x)y′ + my = 0
cₙ₊₁ = (n − m)cₙ / ((n + 1)(n + b))

For every natural degree m and positive rational b, the recurrence terminates above degree m. If c₀ ≠ 0, the resulting polynomial has degree exactly m. With c₀ = 1:

m, bPolynomial solution
2, 11 − 2x + x²/2
3, 21 − 3x/2 + x²/2 − x³/24

The checked equation holds at every rational input, including zero. Both derivative certificates are constructed on bounded rational intervals, and the same polynomial evaluator satisfies the nonzero algebraic relation y − P(x) = 0. The finite Taylor evaluator is proved equal to the existing Horner polynomial evaluator.

This is the Laguerre equation with b = α + 1, normalized by y(0) = c₀. It has a regular singularity at zero and an irregular singularity at infinity, so it is a local Fuchs–Frobenius application.

Painlevé I: local movable poles

y″ = 6y² + x,   s = x − p
y = s⁻² − (p/10)s² − (1/6)s³ + q s⁴ + ⋯

The pole position p and the resonant coefficient q are free rational parameters. Starting from the differential equation, Lean derives the Laurent recurrence, checks its compatibility at the resonant index, and proves that the coefficient algorithm gives the unique formal expansion for these parameters.

Compatibility has content: for the more general equation y″=6y²+f(s), a double-pole Laurent expansion requires the quadratic coefficient of f to vanish. A regression proof rejects f(s)=s².

From a formal expansion to computed derivatives

With R=1+6|p|+6|q|, the coefficients satisfy |cₙ|≤Rⁿ. Set ρ=1/(64R), s=ρt, and U(t)=Σcₙρⁿtⁿ. On rational |t|≤1, the factor U and its first two derivatives are valid nested interval algorithms, each with stage width 4/2ⁿ.

The derivative theorem controls actual box samples: for each rational ε>0, sufficiently small nonzero h and a computed precision depending on h, all endpoint samples v,w and derivative samples d satisfy |w−v−hd|≤ε|h|. This proves the differential meaning of the coefficient shifts.

A finite convolution estimate then proves the nonlinear equation. For any samples u,v,w from the stage-n boxes of U,U′,U″,

|t²w − 4tv + 6u − 6u² − pρ⁴t⁴ − ρ⁵t⁵|
≤ 146 · 2⁻ⁿ.

A positive rational error request gives an explicit stage. These proofs use finite sums, finite differences, and the interval computations of Chapter 1. No completed real-number type or complex-analytic library is introduced.

The pole is genuine

For Y=U/(ρt)² and every stage n≥2, Lean proves

½ ≤ (ρt)² lo(Yₙ) ≤ (ρt)² hi(Yₙ) ≤ 3/2,
0 < |t| ≤ 1.

The computation therefore has a double pole at the freely supplied position p. An exact algebraic identity checks the change of jet coordinates back to the original PI equation.

The derivative certificates are for the regular factor U. Transporting them to Y through the singular coordinate change remains open. These rational local charts do not yet establish complex continuation, cover arbitrary initial data, or exclude every other movable singularity. The global Painlevé property, which excludes movable branch points, is not claimed. These PI charts are not asserted to be algebraic solutions.

Painlevé: equations and algebraic seeds

PI: y″ = 6y² + x
PII(α): y″ = 2y³ + xy + α

In the DLMF convention, PI has no rational constant solution. For PII, the only rational affine solution is y = 0 at α = 0.

The simple-pole candidate y = c/x satisfies PII for every nonzero rational x exactly when α = −c and c ∈ {0, 1, −1}.

What the computation certifies

A polynomial residual evaluated on valid computable complex inputs is valid and respects overlap equivalence. At exact rational inputs its actual interval runtime agrees with the rational result at every stage.

The generic residual interface does not assert that supplied jets are derivatives. The concrete Painleve.AlgebraicSolutionOn package requires those certificates. Every defining algebraic relation includes a nonzero witness, excluding the vacuous equation 0 = 0.

The remaining programme

Next for Fuchs are a general holomorphic-solution bridge, uniform sector bounds and the converse criterion. The Frobenius work still needs analytic derivative certificates and convergence for the remaining nonresonant roots, certified algebraic branches and Puiseux charts, resonant logarithmic solutions, and a separate first-order Fuchs criterion development. For PI, the next steps are the singular-coordinate derivative adapter, complex charts, and continuation and exhaustion toward the global Painlevé property. For PII, the next targets are pole and degree obstructions, Bäcklund transformations and rational-solution classification.

General algebraic-solution classification, PI algebraic nonexistence, monodromy, and PIII–PVI are not proved in this milestone. Formal residuals and conditional jet identities are kept distinct from analytic solutions.
